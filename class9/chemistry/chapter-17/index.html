What are separation techniques? Separation techniques are methods used to separate the components of mixtures based on differences in their physical properties such as boiling point, solubility, particle size, density, volatility, and magnetic properties.

What is the difference between homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures?

  • Homogeneous mixtures: Uniform composition throughout, components are not visibly distinguishable. Examples: air, salt water, sugar solution, alloys.
  • Heterogeneous mixtures: Non-uniform composition with distinct phases or regions. Examples: sand and water, oil and water, granite, muddy water.

What is distillation? Distillation is a technique used to separate components of a liquid mixture by heating and cooling, exploiting differences in volatility and boiling points of the components.

  • Simple distillation: Used when boiling points differ significantly ($>25^\circ C$). Example: Separating ethanol ($78.5^\circ C$) from water ($100^\circ C$).
  • Process: The mixture is heated in a distillation flask. The liquid with the lower boiling point vaporizes first, travels through a condenser (cooled by water), where it condenses back into liquid, and is collected as the distillate. The higher boiling point liquid remains in the flask.
  • Fractional distillation: Used when boiling points are very close (e.g., separation of crude oil components). A fractionating column allows multiple vaporization-condensation cycles for better separation.

What is filtration? Filtration separates an undissolved solid from a liquid by passing the mixture through a filter medium (like filter paper, cloth, or sintered glass).

  • Filtrate: The liquid that passes through the filter paper.
  • Residue: The solid left behind on the filter paper.
  • Example: Separating sand from water.

What is evaporation? Evaporation is used to recover a dissolved solid from a liquid. The solution is heated, the liquid (solvent) vaporizes and escapes, leaving the solid behind.

  • Based on the fact that solids do not vaporize easily (high boiling points) while liquids do (lower boiling points).
  • Example: Obtaining salt from salt water.

What is crystallization? Crystallization is a purification technique that forms pure solid crystals from a saturated solution upon cooling or evaporation. It produces more pure solids than simple evaporation.

  • Used to obtain pure sugar crystals from sugar solution.
  • Examples: Alum crystals, copper sulfate crystals.

What is chromatography? Chromatography separates substances based on their different affinities for a mobile phase (usually a solvent) and a stationary phase (solid or liquid).

  • Mobile phase: The solvent that moves through the stationary phase.
  • Stationary phase: The solid or liquid that remains fixed (e.g., filter paper, silica gel).
  • Components with higher affinity for the mobile phase move faster; those with higher affinity for the stationary phase move slower.
  • Used for separating mixtures of colored compounds (ink dyes, plant pigments) and analyzing chemical samples.

What is sublimation? Sublimation is the process where a solid transforms directly into a gas phase without passing through the liquid state upon heating, and then can deposit back into solid upon cooling.

  • Used to separate a sublimable solid from a non-sublimable solid.
  • Examples: Iodine ($I_2$), naphthalene (mothballs), camphor, ammonium chloride ($NH_4Cl$).
  • When heated, the sublimable substance vaporizes and collects on a cool surface as solid crystals, leaving the other solid behind.

What is magnetic separation? Magnetic separation is used for mixtures containing magnetic materials (like iron, nickel, cobalt).

  • A magnet is passed over the mixture; magnetic particles are attracted to the magnet and separated from non-magnetic components.
  • Example: Separating iron filings from sand or sulfur.

Example - Separating sand and sugar mixture: How to separate a mixture of sand and sugar?

  • Step 1 - Dissolution: Add water to the mixture. Sugar dissolves in water, sand does not.
  • Step 2 - Filtration: Filter the mixture. Sand remains as residue on filter paper; sugar solution (filtrate) passes through.
  • Step 3 - Evaporation/Crystallization: Heat the sugar solution. Water evaporates, leaving behind pure sugar crystals.

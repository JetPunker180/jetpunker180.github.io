Chapter 4, "Cell Cycle," examines the complex processes of cellular reproduction and division. This chapter answers fundamental questions about how cells replicate and divide. What are the phases of the cell cycle? You will learn the detailed stages of interphase including G1 (Gap 1) where organelles replicate and proteins are synthesized, the S phase where DNA replication occurs (chromosomes duplicate), and the G2 phase where final preparations for division take place. You will also learn about the G0 phase (quiescence) where cells like neurons and muscle cells may permanently exit the cycle and stop dividing. What is mitosis and why is it important? The notes provide a deep dive into mitosis - the process of somatic cell division producing two identical diploid daughter cells. You will study all stages: prophase (chromosomes condense, nuclear envelope breaks down), metaphase (chromosomes align at equator), anaphase (sister chromatids separate), and telophase (nuclear envelope reforms). What is the role of the spindle apparatus? You will understand how the spindle apparatus (microtubules) attaches to kinetochores and pulls chromosomes apart during anaphase. What is meiosis and how does it differ from mitosis? A significant portion is dedicated to meiosis I and II (reduction division) which produces four non-identical haploid gametes. You will learn the importance of crossing over and chiasma formation during prophase I for genetic diversity, and how meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes while meiosis II separates sister chromatids. How does cytokinesis differ between plant and animal cells? You will contrast cytokinesis in plants (cell plate formation from Golgi vesicles) versus animals (cleavage furrow from actin microfilaments). What happens when the cell cycle goes wrong? The chapter addresses health topics including cancer (uncontrolled cell division due to loss of cell cycle regulation) and the results of chromosomal non-disjunction (failure of chromosomes to separate properly), including Down syndrome (trisomy 21 where three copies of chromosome 21 are present).

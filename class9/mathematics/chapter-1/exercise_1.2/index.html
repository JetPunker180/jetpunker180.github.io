What are radicals and laws of exponents? Exercise 1.2, "Radicals and Laws of Exponents," focuses on the mathematical tools required to manipulate and simplify expressions involving roots and powers. This exercise covers the fundamental rules for working with radicals and exponents.

How do we convert between radical and exponential forms? You will learn the conversion between radical expressions and exponential forms:

  • Radical form: $\sqrt[n]{a^m}$ (nth root of a raised to power m)
  • Exponential form: $a^{m/n}$
  • Example: $\sqrt[3]{216^2} = (216)^{2/3}$

How do we apply product and quotient rules for radicals? You will learn to apply the product and quotient rules for radicals:

  • Product Rule: $\sqrt[n]{a} \times \sqrt[n]{b} = \sqrt[n]{ab}$ (only when radicals have the same index)
  • Example: $\sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{32}$
  • Quotient Rule: $\frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}} = \sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}}$ (only when radicals have the same index)
  • Important Note: Radicals can only be merged if they have the same index.

What are the laws of exponents? A significant portion of the exercise is dedicated to using laws of exponents:

  • Product Rule: $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$ (only applies to the same base)
  • Quotient Rule: $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$
  • Power Rule: $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$
  • Zero Exponent Rule: $a^0 = 1$ (for $a \neq 0$)
  • Negative Exponent Rule: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ (results in positive exponents in final answer)
  • Fractional Exponent Rule: $a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$

How do we simplify exponential expressions? You will learn to simplify complex numerical and algebraic expressions by reducing bases to prime factors:

  • $216 = 6^3 = (2 \times 3)^3 = 2^3 \times 3^3$
  • $32 = 2^5$
  • $81 = 3^4$
  • $125 = 5^3$
  • Ensuring all final results feature positive exponents (no negative exponents in answer)
  • Combining like terms and grouping similar bases

How do we solve algebraic proofs involving exponents? The exercise includes advanced algebraic proofs where these laws are used to show the equivalence of multi-variable expressions through step-by-step demonstration using exponent rules, grouping similar terms (same base), and careful handling of negative signs outside radicals.

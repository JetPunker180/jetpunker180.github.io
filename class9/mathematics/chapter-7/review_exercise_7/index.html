What is covered in the Miscellaneous Exercise? The Miscellaneous Exercise for Chapter 7 provides a comprehensive review of coordinate geometry, including distance formula, midpoint formula, geometric proofs, and quadrant analysis.

How do we prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram? A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if both pairs of opposite sides are equal AND parallel.

  • Equal sides: Use distance formula to show $AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$
  • Parallel sides: Use slope formula to show slopes of $AB$ and $CD$ are equal, and slopes of $BC$ and $DA$ are equal
  • Slope formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$

How do we find missing coordinates from midpoint data? When a point is the midpoint of two points, set up equations using the midpoint formula.

  • Example: $B(-1,7)$ is the midpoint of $A(3,4)$ and $C(x,y)$
  • $(-1,7) = \left(\frac{3 + x}{2}, \frac{4 + y}{2}\right)$
  • $-1 = \frac{3 + x}{2}$ → $3 + x = -2$ → $x = -5$
  • $7 = \frac{4 + y}{2}$ → $4 + y = 14$ → $y = 10$
  • Therefore, $C(-5, 10)$

How do we find the center of a circle from diameter endpoints? The center of a circle is the midpoint of any diameter.

  • Example: Diameter endpoints are $A(4,5)$ and $B(6,9)$
  • Center $M = \left(\frac{4+6}{2}, \frac{5+9}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{10}{2}, \frac{14}{2}\right) = (5,7)$

How do we determine the quadrant of a point? Quadrants are determined by the signs of $x$ and $y$ coordinates:

  • Quadrant I: $x > 0$, $y > 0$
  • Quadrant II: $x < 0$, $y > 0$
  • Quadrant III: $x < 0$, $y < 0$
  • Quadrant IV: $x > 0$, $y < 0$
  • Example: $(-4, -(-6)) = (-4, 6)$ → $x < 0$, $y > 0$ → Quadrant II

How do we calculate the hypotenuse of a triangle? The hypotenuse is the distance between the endpoints of the longest side (or the side opposite the right angle).

  • Example: For vertices $A(2,3)$, $B(5,7)$, $C(2,10)$
  • $AB = \sqrt{(5-2)^2 + (7-3)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$
  • $BC = \sqrt{(2-5)^2 + (10-7)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$
  • $AC = \sqrt{(2-2)^2 + (10-3)^2} = \sqrt{0 + 49} = 7$
  • If $5^2 + (3\sqrt{2})^2 = 25 + 18 = 43 \neq 49$, not a right triangle. The largest side is $7$.

What is the abscissa on the y-axis? The abscissa (x-coordinate) of any point on the y-axis is always $0$.

How do we find the intersection of diagonals in a kite or square? The intersection point of the diagonals is the midpoint of both diagonals.

  • In a square, diagonals bisect each other at the center
  • Use midpoint formula: $M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$

How do we find missing coordinates when distance is given? Use the distance formula with one coordinate unknown, then solve the resulting equation.

  • Example: Distance between $P(2,3)$ and $Q(x,7)$ is $5$ units
  • $\sqrt{(x-2)^2 + (7-3)^2} = 5$
  • $(x-2)^2 + 16 = 25$ → $(x-2)^2 = 9$ → $x-2 = \pm 3$ → $x = 5$ or $x = -1$

Key formulas to remember:

  • Distance formula: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$
  • Midpoint formula: $M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$
  • Slope formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$

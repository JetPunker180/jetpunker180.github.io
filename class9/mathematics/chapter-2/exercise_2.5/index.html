What are the laws of logarithms? Exercise 2.5, "Laws of Logarithms," focuses on the three fundamental rules used to simplify and manipulate logarithmic expressions.

The Three Laws of Logarithms:

  • Product Law: $\log_a(mn) = \log_a m + \log_a n$ (the log of a product equals the sum of the logs)
  • Quotient Law: $\log_a(m/n) = \log_a m - \log_a n$ (the log of a quotient equals the difference of the logs)
  • Power Law: $\log_a(m^n) = n \log_a m$ (the log of a power equals the exponent times the log of the base)

How do we expand logarithmic expressions? You will learn to expand complex logarithmic expressions by applying the product, quotient, and power laws to terms involving variables and radicals.

Example - Expanding a logarithm: $\log\left(\frac{x^3 y^5}{z^2}\right) = 3\log x + 5\log y - 2\log z$

How do we combine multiple logarithmic terms? You will learn the inverse process of combining multiple logarithmic terms into a single expression with a common base.

Example - Combining logarithms: $2\log_a x + 3\log_a y - \log_a z = \log_a(x^2 y^3 / z)$

What is the change of base formula? The change of base formula allows evaluation of logarithms with non-standard bases: $\log_a b = \frac{\log b}{\log a} = \frac{\ln b}{\ln a}$ (using any common base like 10 or e).

How do we evaluate logarithms using given values? When given specific log values (e.g., $\log_b 2$, $\log_b 3$, $\log_b 5$), you can evaluate complex expressions by breaking them down using the laws of logarithms.

Example - Evaluating using given values: If $\log_b 2 = 0.3010$, $\log_b 3 = 0.4771$, $\log_b 5 = 0.6990$, then:

  • $\log_b 30 = \log_b(2 \times 3 \times 5) = \log_b 2 + \log_b 3 + \log_b 5 = 0.3010 + 0.4771 + 0.6990 = 1.4771$
  • $\log_b(5/2) = \log_b 5 - \log_b 2 = 0.6990 - 0.3010 = 0.3980$
  • $\log_b(36) = \log_b(6^2) = 2\log_b 6 = 2(\log_b 2 + \log_b 3) = 2(0.3010 + 0.4771) = 1.5562$

How do we handle negative logarithmic results (mantissa adjustment)? When a logarithm evaluates to a negative result like $-1.6198$, you must convert it to a positive mantissa using bar notation:

  • $-1.6198 = -2 + 0.3802 = \bar{2}.3802$
  • Rule: Add a negative integer and subtract a positive integer to make the mantissa positive
  • Example: $-1.6198$ → add $-2$ (to go from -1 to -2) and add $+0.3802$ → result is $\bar{2}.3802$

How do we expand logarithms with radicals? For radicals like $\sqrt{x}$, remember that $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$, so $\log(\sqrt{x}) = \frac{1}{2}\log x$.

Important distribution rule: When expanding terms within radicals, distribute the coefficient correctly. For $\log(\sqrt[3]{x^2}) = \log(x^{2/3}) = \frac{2}{3}\log x$.

Common mistakes to avoid:

  • Wrong: $\log_a(m+n) = \log_a m + \log_a n$ (No such law exists!)
  • Wrong: $\log_a(mn) = \log_a m \times \log_a n$ (This is incorrect)
  • Correct: $\log_a(mn) = \log_a m + \log_a n$

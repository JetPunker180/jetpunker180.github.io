What are common logarithms? Exercise 2.3, "Common Logarithms," focuses on the practical computation of base-10 logarithms for various types of real numbers. Common logarithms are logarithms with base 10, written as $\log x$ (base 10 is implied).

What are the two parts of a logarithm? Every logarithm can be broken down into two essential parts:

  • Characteristic: The integral part (integer part) of the logarithm, determined by the number's magnitude (how many digits before decimal)
  • Mantissa: The fractional part (decimal part), always positive, found using logarithm tables

How do we find the characteristic for numbers greater than 1?

  • For numbers $\geq 1$, characteristic = (number of digits before decimal) - 1
  • Examples: $\log 5313$: 5313 has 4 digits → characteristic = $4 - 1 = 3$
  • $\log 4580$: 4580 has 4 digits → characteristic = $4 - 1 = 3$
  • $\log 4$: 4 has 1 digit → characteristic = $1 - 1 = 0$

How do we find the characteristic for numbers between 0 and 1 (decimals)?

  • For decimals, the characteristic is negative
  • Characteristic = number of zeros after decimal before first non-zero digit, with negative sign
  • $\log 0.01207$: Has one zero after decimal before 1 → characteristic = -2 (written as $\bar{2}$ in bar notation)
  • $\log 0.00004$: Has four zeros after decimal before 4 → characteristic = -5 (written as $\bar{5}$ in bar notation)

What is bar notation? Bar notation is used to represent negative characteristics:

  • Example: $\log 0.01207 = \bar{2}.0817$ (read as "bar 2 point 0817")
  • Bar notation keeps the mantissa positive while showing the characteristic is negative
  • This is different from $-2.0817$ (which would have a negative mantissa)

What is the logarithm of one trillion? $1 \text{ trillion} = 1,000,000,000,000 = 10^{12}$, so $\log(10^{12}) = 12$ (characteristic = 12, mantissa = 0)

Why is the logarithm of a negative number undefined? The logarithmic function $\log_a x$ is only defined for $x > 0$ (positive real numbers). This is because:

  • There is no real number $y$ such that $10^y = -4$ (any real exponent gives a positive result)
  • $\log(-4)$ is impossible/undefined because the domain of the logarithm function is $(0, \infty)$

How do we use mantissa values? The mantissa is found from logarithm tables. For example, the mantissa for 5313 is found by looking up 5313 in log tables (or using calculator).

Key rules to remember:

  • Characteristic is positive for numbers $>1$
  • Characteristic is negative (bar notation) for numbers $<1$
  • Characteristic is zero for numbers between 1 and 10 (e.g., $\log 4$ has characteristic 0)
  • Logarithms are only defined for positive real numbers
  • $\log 1 = 0$ for any valid base

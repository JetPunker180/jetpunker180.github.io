What is scientific notation? Exercise 2.1, "Scientific Notation," introduces the standard method for expressing very large or very small numbers in the form $a \times 10^n$, where the coefficient $a$ is between 1 and 10 ($1 \leq a < 10$) and $n$ is an integer exponent.

Why do we use scientific notation? Scientific notation allows us to write extremely large numbers (like distance to stars) and extremely small numbers (like size of atoms) concisely, making calculations easier and reducing errors.

How do we convert large numbers to scientific notation? You will learn step-by-step conversion:

  • Large integer example: $53,400,000 = 5.34 \times 10^7$ (move decimal 7 places to the left, positive exponent)
  • Rule for large numbers: Count how many places the decimal moves left → that becomes the positive exponent

How do we convert small decimals to scientific notation?

  • Small decimal example: $0.00053407 = 5.3407 \times 10^{-4}$ (move decimal 4 places to the right, negative exponent)
  • Rule for small numbers: Count how many places the decimal moves right → that becomes the negative exponent

How do we convert scientific notation back to standard notation? For positive exponents, move decimal right. For negative exponents, move decimal left.

How do we simplify expressions using scientific notation and laws of exponents? A significant portion of the exercise focuses on simplifying complex expressions involving products and quotients:

  • Product Rule: $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$
  • Quotient Rule: $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$
  • Power Rule: $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$
  • Zero Exponent Rule: $a^0 = 1$ (for $a \neq 0$)
  • Negative Exponent Rule: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$

How do we solve real-world word problems using scientific notation? The exercise applies these concepts to real-world problems:

Example 1 - Time Conversion Problem: Calculate the total time required for one million recitations if each recitation takes 2 seconds.

  • Total seconds = $1,000,000 \times 2 = 2 \times 10^6$ seconds
  • Convert to minutes: $2 \times 10^6 \div 60 = 3.33 \times 10^4$ minutes
  • Convert to hours: $3.33 \times 10^4 \div 60 = 5.56 \times 10^2$ hours
  • Convert to days: $5.56 \times 10^2 \div 24 \approx 23.15$ days
  • Key conversion factors: $1 \text{ hour} = 3600 \text{ seconds}$, $1 \text{ day} = 86400 \text{ seconds}$

Example 2 - Speed of Light Problem: Determine the time it takes sunlight to reach Earth.

  • Speed of light: $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$
  • Distance from Sun to Earth: $d = 1.5 \times 10^{11} \text{ m}$
  • Time formula: $t = \frac{d}{c} = \frac{1.5 \times 10^{11}}{3 \times 10^8} = 0.5 \times 10^3 = 500 \text{ seconds}$
  • Convert to minutes: $500 \div 60 \approx 8.33 \text{ minutes}$

What is the coefficient rule? The coefficient $a$ must satisfy $1 \leq a < 10$ (exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point).

Common mistakes to avoid:

  • Wrong: $53400000 = 534 \times 10^5$ (coefficient 534 is not between 1 and 10)
  • Correct: $53400000 = 5.34 \times 10^7$ (coefficient 5.34 is between 1 and 10)

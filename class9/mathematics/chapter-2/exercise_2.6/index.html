What are the applications of logarithms? Exercise 2.6, "Applications of Logarithms," demonstrates the practical power of logarithmic functions in solving mathematical and real-world problems.

How do we find the number of digits in a large number? You will learn to find the number of digits in exceptionally large numbers like $3^{30}$ or $100^{100}$ using logarithms:

  • Step 1: Find $\log N$ (where $N = a^n$)
  • Step 2: Identify the characteristic (integer part) of $\log N$
  • Step 3: Number of digits = Characteristic + 1

Example - Finding digits in $3^{30}$:

  • $\log 3^{30} = 30 \times \log 3 = 30 \times 0.4771 = 14.313$
  • Characteristic = 14
  • Number of digits = $14 + 1 = 15$ digits

Example - Finding digits in $100^{100}$:

  • $\log 100^{100} = 100 \times \log 100 = 100 \times 2 = 200$
  • Characteristic = 200
  • Number of digits = $200 + 1 = 201$ digits

How do we evaluate complex expressions using logarithms? You will learn to evaluate intricate numerical expressions featuring multiple operations and radicals:

  • Step 1: Take log of the entire expression
  • Step 2: Apply laws of logarithms (product, quotient, power)
  • Step 3: Simplify using given log values or log tables
  • Step 4: Take antilog to get the final value

Example - Evaluating $\sqrt[3]{\frac{45 \times 32}{128}}$:

  • Let $x = \sqrt[3]{\frac{45 \times 32}{128}} = \left(\frac{1440}{128}\right)^{1/3} = (11.25)^{1/3}$
  • $\log x = \frac{1}{3} \log 11.25 = \frac{1}{3} \times 1.05115 = 0.35038$
  • $x = \text{antilog}(0.35038) \approx 2.24$

How do we handle negative logarithmic results (making mantissa positive)? When a logarithm evaluates to a negative result, you must convert it to a positive mantissa for antilog calculation:

  • Example: $-1.4813$
  • Add a negative integer and subtract a positive integer: $-1.4813 = -2 + 0.5187$
  • Result: $\bar{2}.5187$ (read as "bar 2 point 5187")
  • Rule: The mantissa must always be positive between 0 and 1

How do we convert negative numbers to positive mantissas?

  • $-0.2345 = -1 + 0.7655 = \bar{1}.7655$
  • $-2.1234 = -3 + 0.8766 = \bar{3}.8766$
  • $-3.9876 = -4 + 0.0124 = \bar{4}.0124$

What is the Richter scale and how does it use logarithms? The Richter scale measures earthquake magnitude using logarithmic function:

  • Richter scale formula: $M = \log\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)$
  • Where $M$ = magnitude, $I$ = earthquake intensity, $I_0$ = reference intensity
  • Each whole number increase represents a 10× increase in amplitude and ~31.6× increase in energy

Example - Comparing earthquake intensities: An earthquake of magnitude 6.0 is how many times stronger than magnitude 4.0?

  • $M_1 = \log(I_1/I_0) = 6.0$
  • $M_2 = \log(I_2/I_0) = 4.0$
  • Subtract: $\log(I_1/I_0) - \log(I_2/I_0) = 6.0 - 4.0 = 2.0$
  • $\log(I_1/I_2) = 2.0$
  • $I_1/I_2 = 10^2 = 100$ times stronger in amplitude
  • Energy ratio: $10^{1.5 \times 2} = 10^3 = 1000$ times more energy

Example - Finding intensity ratio from magnitudes: If one earthquake measures 7.2 and another measures 5.6, find the intensity ratio.

  • $M_1 - M_2 = 7.2 - 5.6 = 1.6$
  • $\log(I_1/I_2) = 1.6$
  • $I_1/I_2 = \text{antilog}(1.6) \approx 39.8$ times stronger

Key formulas to remember:

  • Number of digits in $N$ = Characteristic of $\log N + 1$
  • For negative logs, convert to bar notation: $-n.xxxx = \overline{n+1}.(1 - xxxx)$
  • Richter scale: $M = \log(I/I_0)$, $I/I_0 = 10^M$
  • Energy released: $E \propto 10^{1.5M}$

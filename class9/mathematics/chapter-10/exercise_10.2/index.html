How do we demonstrate the concurrency of triangle lines? Angle bisectors, altitudes, perpendicular bisectors, and medians are concurrent, meaning they all intersect at a single unique point within or outside a triangle.

Where do these points lie in different triangle types? In a right triangle, the orthocenter is at the vertex of the right angle, and the circumcenter is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. In an obtuse triangle, both the circumcenter and orthocenter lie outside the figure.

  • Identify the Incenter as the point where angle bisectors meet.
  • Locate the Orthocenter at the intersection of altitudes.
  • Find the Circumcenter where the perpendicular (right) bisectors of the sides intersect.
  • Determine the Centroid using medians; it divides each median in a 2:1 ratio.
  • Recognize that in isosceles triangles, the incenter and centroid lie on the same line—the median from the apex.

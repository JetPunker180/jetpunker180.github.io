How do we find the angle between two lines? The angle $\theta$ between two lines with slopes $m_1$ and $m_2$ is given by the formula:

  • $\tan \theta = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{1 + m_2 m_1}$
  • If the denominator $1 + m_2 m_1 = 0$, then $\tan \theta$ is undefined, so $\theta = 90^\circ$ (lines are perpendicular)
  • The formula gives the acute or obtuse angle depending on the order of slopes

What is the correct order of slopes in the formula? The formula $\tan \theta = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{1 + m_2 m_1}$ gives the angle from the line with slope $m_1 to the line with slope $m_2$ (counterclockwise).

  • For the acute angle between lines, take the absolute value: $\tan \theta = \left|\frac{m_2 - m_1}{1 + m_2 m_1}\right|$

Example - Angle between two lines: Find the angle between lines with slopes $m_1 = 2$ and $m_2 = -3$.

  • $\tan \theta = \frac{-3 - 2}{1 + (-3)(2)} = \frac{-5}{1 - 6} = \frac{-5}{-5} = 1$
  • $\theta = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$

How do we handle negative angle results? If the calculated $\tan \theta$ is negative, the angle from the formula is negative (indicating the clockwise direction). To find the acute angle, take the absolute value. To find the obtuse angle (if needed), subtract the acute angle from $180^\circ$.

  • Example: If $\tan \theta = -0.5$, acute angle = $\tan^{-1}(0.5) \approx 26.6^\circ$
  • The lines also form an obtuse angle of $180^\circ - 26.6^\circ = 153.4^\circ$

How do we find interior angles of a triangle? For triangle $ABC$, find slopes of sides $AB$, $BC$, and $CA$:

  • $\angle A$ is the angle between $AB$ and $AC$ (use slopes $m_{AB}$ and $m_{AC}$)
  • $\angle B$ is the angle between $BA$ and $BC$ (use slopes $m_{BA}$ and $m_{BC}$)
  • $\angle C$ is the angle between $CA$ and $CB$ (use slopes $m_{CA}$ and $m_{CB}$)
  • Verify that $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$

Example - Interior angles of triangle: For $A(1,2)$, $B(4,6)$, $C(7,2)$

  • $m_{AB} = (6-2)/(4-1) = 4/3 \approx 1.333$
  • $m_{AC} = (2-2)/(7-1) = 0/6 = 0$ (horizontal)
  • $\tan \angle A = \left|\frac{0 - 4/3}{1 + 0}\right| = \left|-4/3\right| = 4/3$ → $\angle A \approx 53.13^\circ$
  • $m_{BA} = (2-6)/(1-4) = -4/-3 = 4/3$, $m_{BC} = (2-6)/(7-4) = -4/3$
  • $\tan \angle B = \left|\frac{-4/3 - 4/3}{1 + (-4/3)(4/3)}\right| = \left|\frac{-8/3}{1 - 16/9}\right| = \left|\frac{-8/3}{-7/9}\right| = \frac{8/3}{7/9} = \frac{72}{21} = \frac{24}{7} \approx 3.428$ → $\angle B \approx 73.74^\circ$
  • $\angle C = 180^\circ - 53.13^\circ - 73.74^\circ = 53.13^\circ$ (isosceles triangle)

How do we find the point of intersection of two lines? Solve the two linear equations simultaneously.

  • Method 1 - Elimination: Add or subtract equations to eliminate one variable
  • Method 2 - Substitution: Solve for one variable in terms of the other, then substitute

Example - Finding intersection point: Solve $2x + y + 1 = 0$ and $x - y - 4 = 0$.

  • Add equations: $(2x + y + 1) + (x - y - 4) = 0 + 0$ → $3x - 3 = 0$ → $x = 1$
  • Substitute $x = 1$ into $x - y - 4 = 0$: $1 - y - 4 = 0$ → $-y - 3 = 0$ → $y = -3$
  • Intersection point: $(1, -3)$

How do we find the equation of a line passing through an intersection point? After finding the intersection point $(x_1, y_1)$, use the point-slope form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ where $m$ is determined by given conditions (parallel, perpendicular, or specific value).

Example - Line through intersection: Find equation of line through intersection of $2x + y + 1 = 0$ and $x - y - 4 = 0$ (which is $(1, -3)$) parallel to $3x + 4y = 0$.

  • Slope of given line: $3x + 4y = 0$ → $4y = -3x$ → $y = -\frac{3}{4}x$, so $m = -\frac{3}{4}$
  • Parallel line has same slope: $m = -\frac{3}{4}$
  • Through $(1, -3)$: $y - (-3) = -\frac{3}{4}(x - 1)$
  • $y + 3 = -\frac{3}{4}x + \frac{3}{4}$ → $y = -\frac{3}{4}x - \frac{9}{4}$ → Multiply by 4: $4y = -3x - 9$ → $3x + 4y + 9 = 0$

How do we find the angle between lines given in general form? First convert to slope-intercept form to find slopes, then apply the angle formula.

Important note: When calculating interior angles of a triangle, ensure you use the slopes in the correct order (the angle at a vertex is between the two sides meeting at that vertex).

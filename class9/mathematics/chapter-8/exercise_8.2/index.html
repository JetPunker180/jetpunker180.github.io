What are the equations for horizontal and vertical lines? Special orientation lines have simple equations:

  • Horizontal lines: Slope $m = 0$, equation $y = y_1$ (constant y-value)
  • Vertical lines: Slope undefined ($m = 1/0$), equation $x = x_1$ (constant x-value)
  • Example: Line through $(\frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4})$ → horizontal: $y = \frac{3}{4}$, vertical: $x = \frac{1}{4}$

What is the slope-intercept form? When the slope $m$ and y-intercept $c$ are known:

  • $y = mx + c$
  • $c$ is the y-coordinate where the line crosses the y-axis ($x = 0$)

What is the point-slope form? When a point $(x_1, y_1)$ on the line and the slope $m$ are known:

  • $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$
  • This is the most versatile form for finding line equations

How do we find equations of parallel and perpendicular lines? Use the point-slope form with slope conditions:

  • Parallel lines: $m_2 = m_1$ (slopes equal)
  • Perpendicular lines: $m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$ (negative reciprocal)
  • Example: Line through $(2,3)$ parallel to line with slope $4$ → $y - 3 = 4(x - 2)$ → $y = 4x - 5$
  • Example: Line through $(2,3)$ perpendicular to line with slope $4$ → $m = -\frac{1}{4}$ → $y - 3 = -\frac{1}{4}(x - 2)$

What is the symmetric form of a straight line? When a point $(x_1, y_1)$ and the inclination angle $\theta$ are known:

  • $\frac{x - x_1}{\cos \theta} = \frac{y - y_1}{\sin \theta}$
  • This uses the direction ratios derived from the angle of inclination
  • Example: Line through $(1,2)$ with inclination $60^\circ$ → $\frac{x-1}{\cos 60^\circ} = \frac{y-2}{\sin 60^\circ}$ → $\frac{x-1}{1/2} = \frac{y-2}{\sqrt{3}/2}$

What is the normal form of a straight line? The normal form uses the perpendicular distance from the origin:

  • $x \cos \theta + y \sin \theta = p$
  • Where $p$ is the perpendicular distance from the origin to the line (always $p \geq 0$)
  • $\theta$ is the angle the perpendicular makes with the positive x-axis ($0 \leq \theta < 2\pi$)

How do we reduce a general equation to normal form? For $Ax + By + C = 0$ (with $C$ moved to RHS):

  • $Ax + By = -C$
  • Divide both sides by $\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$: $\frac{A}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}x + \frac{B}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}y = \frac{-C}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}$
  • Compare with $x \cos \theta + y \sin \theta = p$
  • Example: $6x + 8y - 11 = 0$ → $6x + 8y = 11$
  • $\sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$
  • $\frac{6}{10}x + \frac{8}{10}y = \frac{11}{10}$ → $\cos \theta = 0.6$, $\sin \theta = 0.8$, $p = 1.1$
  • Normal form: $x \cos \theta + y \sin \theta = p$ where $\theta \approx 53.13^\circ$

How do we find the median of a triangle? A median goes from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

  • Step 1: Find midpoint of opposite side: $M = \left(\frac{x_2 + x_3}{2}, \frac{y_2 + y_3}{2}\right)$
  • Step 2: Use two-point form or point-slope form with vertex and midpoint
  • Example: For triangle $A(1,2)$, $B(3,4)$, $C(5,6)$: midpoint of $BC = (4,5)$, median from $A$ passes through $(1,2)$ and $(4,5)$

How do we find the altitude of a triangle? An altitude goes from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side.

  • Step 1: Find slope of opposite side: $m_{BC} = \frac{y_3 - y_2}{x_3 - x_2}$
  • Step 2: Perpendicular slope: $m_{alt} = -\frac{1}{m_{BC}}$
  • Step 3: Use point-slope form with vertex: $y - y_1 = m_{alt}(x - x_1)$
  • Example: $A(1,2)$, $B(3,4)$, $C(5,6)$: $m_{BC} = (6-4)/(5-3) = 2/2 = 1$, $m_{alt} = -1$
  • Altitude from $A$: $y - 2 = -1(x - 1)$ → $y = -x + 3$

Important formulas summary:

  • Horizontal line: $y = y_1$
  • Vertical line: $x = x_1$
  • Slope-intercept: $y = mx + c$
  • Point-slope: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$
  • Two-point form: $\frac{y - y_1}{y_2 - y_1} = \frac{x - x_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
  • Symmetric form: $\frac{x - x_1}{\cos \theta} = \frac{y - y_1}{\sin \theta}$
  • Normal form: $x \cos \theta + y \sin \theta = p$

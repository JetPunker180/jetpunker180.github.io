What is factorization of polynomials? Exercise 4.1, "Factorization of Polynomials," introduces the core techniques for breaking down complex algebraic expressions into simpler factors (products of irreducible polynomials).

How do we factorize by taking common factors? The simplest method is identifying common factors in all terms:

  • $ab + ac = a(b + c)$
  • $4x^2 + 6x = 2x(2x + 3)$
  • Always take the greatest common factor (GCF) first

How do we factorize by grouping? For polynomials with four or more terms, group them into pairs:

  • $ac + bc + ad + bd = (ac + bc) + (ad + bd) = c(a + b) + d(a + b) = (a + b)(c + d)$
  • Example: $x^3 + 2x^2 + 3x + 6 = x^2(x + 2) + 3(x + 2) = (x + 2)(x^2 + 3)$

What are the key algebraic identities for factorization? These formulas help factorize special forms:

  • Square of sum: $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
  • Square of difference: $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
  • Difference of squares: $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$
  • Sum of cubes: $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$
  • Difference of cubes: $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$

How do we factorize by middle-term breaking (splitting the middle term)? For quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c$:

  • Find two numbers whose product = $ac$ and sum = $b$
  • Split middle term into these two numbers
  • Factor by grouping

Example - Middle-term breaking: Factor $6x^2 - 7x - 5$

  • $a = 6$, $b = -7$, $c = -5$
  • $ac = 6 \times (-5) = -30$
  • Find numbers: product = $-30$, sum = $-7$ → numbers are $-10$ and $+3$
  • $6x^2 - 10x + 3x - 5 = 2x(3x - 5) + 1(3x - 5) = (3x - 5)(2x + 1)$
  • Careful with negative signs: For $6 - 7x - 5x^2$, rewrite in standard form $-5x^2 - 7x + 6$ first

What is completing the square method? Some expressions are factorized by adding and subtracting a term to form a perfect square:

  • For $x^4 + 4m^4$: Add and subtract $4x^2m^2$
  • $x^4 + 4x^2m^2 + 4m^4 - 4x^2m^2 = (x^2 + 2m^2)^2 - (2xm)^2$
  • Then apply difference of squares: $(x^2 + 2m^2 - 2xm)(x^2 + 2m^2 + 2xm)$
  • Key: Identify the middle term $2ab$ to complete the square correctly

How do we find missing factors? If one factor is given, divide the polynomial by that factor to find the other factor(s).

How do we represent expressions as difference of two squares? Some expressions can be rewritten as $P^2 - Q^2$ even if not obviously squares:

  • Add and subtract appropriate terms to form perfect squares
  • Then factor as $(P + Q)(P - Q)$

Common mistakes to avoid:

  • Forgetting to fully factor: check if any factor can be factorized further
  • Sign errors in middle-term breaking: always verify sum equals $b$
  • Handling negative coefficients: rearrange in standard form first
  • Missing the GCF before applying other methods

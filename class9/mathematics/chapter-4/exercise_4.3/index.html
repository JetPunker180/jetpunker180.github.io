What is HCF of polynomials? Exercise 4.3, "HCF of Polynomials," focuses on identifying the Highest Common Factor among various algebraic expressions. The Highest Common Factor (HCF) is the largest expression that divides each given expression completely.

How do we find HCF of monomials? The exercise is divided into two main sections:

  • HCF of Monomials: Break each monomial into numerical coefficients and variable parts (literal factors like p, q, r)
  • HCF by Factorization: Factorize polynomials first, then find common factors

HCF of Monomials - Method:

  • Find HCF of numerical coefficients (using normal number HCF)
  • For each variable, take the smallest power that appears in all monomials
  • Example: Find HCF of $12p^3q^2r$ and $18p^2q^3r^2$
  • Numerical: HCF of 12 and 18 = 6
  • p: smallest power is $p^2$
  • q: smallest power is $q^2$
  • r: smallest power is $r$ (since first has $r^1$, second has $r^2$)
  • HCF = $6p^2q^2r$

What is the rule for powers in HCF of monomials? For each variable, the HCF contains the smallest exponent present in any monomial (e.g., $p^2$ is HCF of $p^3$ and $p^2$, since $p^2$ divides both).

How do we find HCF by factorization? When polynomials are not monomials:

  • Step 1: Factorize each polynomial completely using algebraic identities
  • Step 2: Identify common factors present in all factorizations
  • Step 3: Multiply these common factors to get HCF

What algebraic identities are used for factorization?

  • Difference of squares: $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$
  • Sum of cubes: $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$
  • Difference of cubes: $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$
  • Square of sum: $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
  • Square of difference: $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$

How does middle-term breaking help in HCF? For quadratic trinomials like $x^2 - 4x - 21$:

  • Factorize: Find two numbers whose product = $-21$ and sum = $-4$
  • Numbers are $-7$ and $+3$
  • $x^2 - 7x + 3x - 21 = x(x - 7) + 3(x - 7) = (x - 7)(x + 3)$
  • Then find HCF with other factorized polynomials

How do we handle grouping for HCF? For polynomials with four or more terms, factor by grouping first:

  • Group terms with common factors
  • Factor each group
  • Take out common binomial factor
  • Then find HCF with other expressions

What is the HCF of identical polynomials? If two polynomials are exactly the same, their HCF is the polynomial itself (e.g., HCF of $(x+2)(x+3)$ and $(x+2)(x+3)$ is $(x+2)(x+3)$).

How do we reconstruct polynomials from common and uncommon factors? Given the HCF and the two polynomials, you can find the missing factors by dividing.

Example - Finding HCF of three polynomials: Find HCF of $x^3 - 1$, $x^4 - 1$, and $x^3 - 2x^2 + x - 2$

  • Factorize each: $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)$
  • $x^4 - 1 = (x^2 - 1)(x^2 + 1) = (x - 1)(x + 1)(x^2 + 1)$
  • $x^3 - 2x^2 + x - 2 = x^2(x - 2) + 1(x - 2) = (x - 2)(x^2 + 1)$
  • Common factor: None? Check carefully - $x^3 - 1$ has $(x-1)$ but not $x^2+1$
  • If no common factor, HCF is 1

Key points to remember for HCF:

  • HCF is always a factor of each expression
  • For monomials: numerical HCF × product of smallest powers of variables
  • For polynomials: factorize completely first, then identify common factors
  • If expressions are identical, HCF = the expression
  • If no common factors, HCF = 1

Common mistakes to avoid:

  • Forgetting to factorize completely before finding HCF
  • Incorrect sign handling in middle-term breaking (especially with negative numbers)
  • Assuming HCF is always visible without factorization
  • Missing common factors when variables have different powers

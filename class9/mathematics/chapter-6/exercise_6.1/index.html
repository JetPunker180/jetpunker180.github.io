What is the sexagesimal system? The sexagesimal system is a base-60 system used for measuring angles. In this system:

  • $1^\circ$ (degree) = $60'$ (minutes)
  • $1'$ (minute) = $60''$ (seconds)
  • $1^\circ = 60 \times 60 = 3600''$ (seconds)

How do we convert sexagesimal measures to seconds? Multiply degrees by 3600, minutes by 60, then add them together.

  • Example: $10^\circ 30'$
  • Degrees to seconds: $10 \times 3600 = 36,000''$
  • Minutes to seconds: $30 \times 60 = 1,800''$
  • Total: $36,000 + 1,800 = 37,800''$

How do we convert seconds to $D^\circ M' S''$ form? Divide seconds by 3600 to get degrees, the remainder gives minutes and seconds.

How do we convert $D^\circ M' S''$ to decimal degrees? Divide minutes by 60 and seconds by 3600, then add to the degrees.

  • Example: $45^\circ 45' 45''$
  • Minutes to degrees: $45' = \frac{45}{60} = 0.75^\circ$
  • Seconds to degrees: $45'' = \frac{45}{3600} = 0.0125^\circ$
  • Total decimal degrees: $45 + 0.75 + 0.0125 = 45.7625^\circ$

How do we convert decimal degrees to $D^\circ M' S''$ form?

  • Step 1: The whole number part is the degrees
  • Step 2: Multiply the decimal part by 60 → the whole number part is minutes
  • Step 3: Multiply the remaining decimal by 60 → the result is seconds
  • Example: Convert $45.7625^\circ$ to $D^\circ M' S''$
  • Degrees = $45^\circ$
  • $0.7625 \times 60 = 45.75$ → minutes = $45'$
  • $0.75 \times 60 = 45$ → seconds = $45''$
  • Result: $45^\circ 45' 45''$

How do we convert degrees to radians? Use the conversion factor: $1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180}$ radians.

  • Formula: $\text{Radians} = \text{Degrees} \times \frac{\pi}{180}$
  • Example: $150^\circ = 150 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{150\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$ radians
  • Example with $\pi = 3.1416$: $270^\circ = 270 \times \frac{3.1416}{180} = 1.5 \times 3.1416 = 4.7124$ radians

How do we convert radians to degrees? Use the conversion factor: $1 \text{ radian} = \frac{180}{\pi}$ degrees.

  • Formula: $\text{Degrees} = \text{Radians} \times \frac{180}{\pi}$
  • Example: $1 \text{ rad} = 1 \times \frac{180}{\pi} \approx \frac{180}{3.1416} \approx 57.2958^\circ$
  • Convert to $D^\circ M' S''$:
  • Degrees = $57^\circ$
  • $0.2958 \times 60 = 17.748$ → minutes = $17'$
  • $0.748 \times 60 = 44.88$ → seconds $\approx 45''$
  • Result: $1 \text{ rad} \approx 57^\circ 17' 45''$

What are the common angle conversions to remember?

  • $\pi$ radians = $180^\circ$
  • $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians = $90^\circ$
  • $\frac{\pi}{3}$ radians = $60^\circ$
  • $\frac{\pi}{4}$ radians = $45^\circ$
  • $\frac{\pi}{6}$ radians = $30^\circ$

Important rounding note: When using $\pi = 3.1416$, round final answers to 4 decimal places as required by FBISE exam instructions.

Common mistake to avoid: Mixing up the conversion factors. Remember: degrees → radians = multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$ (smaller number); radians → degrees = multiply by $\frac{180}{\pi}$ (larger number).

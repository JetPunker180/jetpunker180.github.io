What are three-figure bearings? Bearings are a standard way to describe direction in navigation and surveying.

  • Bearings are measured clockwise from North ($000^\circ$)
  • Always expressed as a three-digit figure (e.g., $050^\circ$, $240^\circ$, $315^\circ$)
  • North = $000^\circ$ or $360^\circ$, East = $090^\circ$, South = $180^\circ$, West = $270^\circ$

How do we calculate reciprocal bearings (back-bearings)? The reciprocal bearing is the bearing from the destination back to the starting point.

  • Formula: Reciprocal bearing = bearing $\pm 180^\circ$
  • If the original bearing is $< 180^\circ$, add $180^\circ$
  • If the original bearing is $\geq 180^\circ$, subtract $180^\circ$
  • Always adjust to fall within $0^\circ \leq \text{bearing} < 360^\circ$

Example - Reciprocal bearing: If the bearing of B from A is $250^\circ$, find the bearing of A from B.

  • Since $250^\circ \geq 180^\circ$, subtract $180^\circ$: $250^\circ - 180^\circ = 70^\circ$
  • Write as three-digit: $070^\circ$
  • Therefore, bearing of A from B is $070^\circ$

How do we find total travel distance using Pythagorean theorem? When two legs of a journey are perpendicular (right angle), the direct distance is the hypotenuse.

  • Example: A ship travels 12 km east, then 5 km north. Find the direct distance.
  • Apply Pythagorean theorem: $d = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ km

How do we find unknown distances using trigonometric ratios? In navigation triangles, use $\tan \theta$, $\sin \theta$, or $\cos \theta$ based on known sides.

  • Example: If $\cos \theta = \frac{3}{5}$, then $\theta = \cos^{-1}(\frac{3}{5}) \approx 53.13^\circ$
  • Example: In a right triangle, if adjacent = 50 m and $\tan 32^\circ = 0.6249$, opposite = $50 \times 0.6249 = 31.245$ m

How do we resolve bearing vectors into northward and eastward components? For a journey of distance $d$ at bearing $\theta$ (measured clockwise from North):

  • Northward component: $N = d \cos \theta$
  • Eastward component: $E = d \sin \theta$
  • Example: A ship travels 13 km on a bearing of $040^\circ$.
  • $N = 13 \times \cos 40^\circ = 13 \times 0.7660 = 9.96$ km north
  • $E = 13 \times \sin 40^\circ = 13 \times 0.6428 = 8.36$ km east

How do we find the bearing between two points? Use inverse trigonometric functions after calculating components.

  • Let $\Delta E$ = eastward displacement, $\Delta N$ = northward displacement
  • First quadrant ($\Delta E > 0$, $\Delta N > 0$): $\theta = \tan^{-1}(\frac{\Delta E}{\Delta N})$
  • Adjust based on quadrant for other cases

How do we solve multi-stage path problems? Model each leg as a vector, find total northward and eastward displacement, then calculate resultant distance and bearing.

  • Example: Stage 1: 10 km at $060^\circ$ → $N_1 = 10\cos60^\circ = 5$, $E_1 = 10\sin60^\circ = 8.66$
  • Stage 2: 8 km at $120^\circ$ → $N_2 = 8\cos120^\circ = 8 \times (-0.5) = -4$, $E_2 = 8\sin120^\circ = 8 \times 0.866 = 6.93$
  • Total $N = 5 - 4 = 1$ km, Total $E = 8.66 + 6.93 = 15.59$ km
  • Resultant distance = \sqrt{1^2 + 15.59^2} \approx 15.62$ km
  • Bearing = \tan^{-1}(\frac{15.59}{1}) \approx 86.3^\circ$

What are the key bearing values?

  • North = $000^\circ$ or $360^\circ$
  • North-East = $045^\circ$
  • East = $090^\circ$
  • South-East = $135^\circ$
  • South = $180^\circ$
  • South-West = $225^\circ$
  • West = $270^\circ$
  • North-West = $315^\circ$

Common mistake to avoid: Always ensure bearings are written as three digits (e.g., $070^\circ$ not $70^\circ$).

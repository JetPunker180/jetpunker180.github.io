What is a sector of a circle? A sector is a region bounded by two radii and the intercepted arc of a circle. It resembles a "slice" of pie.

What are the key formulas for sector?

  • Arc length: $l = r\theta$ (where $\theta$ is in radians)
  • Sector area: $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ (where $\theta$ is in radians)
  • Perimeter of sector: $P = 2r + l = 2r + r\theta$

How do we find arc length? Multiply the radius by the central angle in radians.

  • Example: $r = 5$ cm, $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$ rad
  • $l = 5 \times \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{5\pi}{3} \approx 5.24$ cm

How do we find sector area? Multiply half the square of the radius by the central angle in radians.

  • Example: $r = 5$ cm, $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$ rad
  • $A = \frac{1}{2} \times 5^2 \times \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{25\pi}{6} \approx 13.09$ cm²

How do we solve for unknown radius or angle? Rearrange the formulas:

  • To find angle: $\theta = \frac{l}{r}$
  • To find radius from arc length: $r = \frac{l}{\theta}$
  • To find radius from area: $r = \sqrt{\frac{2A}{\theta}}$
  • To find angle from area: $\theta = \frac{2A}{r^2}$

Why must we convert degrees to radians before using formulas? The formulas $l = r\theta$ and $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ are derived assuming $\theta$ is in radians. Using degrees directly will give wrong answers.

  • Example: Convert $75^\circ$ to radians: $75 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{12}$ rad
  • Then use this radian value in formulas

How do we calculate the perimeter of a half-circle? A half-circle is a sector with $\theta = \pi$ radians.

  • Arc length: $l = r\pi$
  • Diameter: $d = 2r$
  • Perimeter = $l + d = r\pi + 2r$

How do we apply sector formulas to pendulum motion? A swinging pendulum sweeps out a circular arc. The distance traveled by the pendulum bob is the arc length.

  • $l = r\theta$ where $r$ is the pendulum length, $\theta$ is the swing angle in radians
  • Also related to speed and time: $l = v \times t$
  • Example: Pendulum length $r = 1$ m, swing angle $30^\circ = \pi/6$ rad, arc length $l = 1 \times \pi/6 = \pi/6 \approx 0.524$ m

How do we model a vehicle turning on a circular curve? The distance traveled along the curve equals the arc length.

  • $l = r\theta$ where $r$ is the radius of the curve, $\theta$ is the turning angle in radians
  • Given speed $v$ and time $t$, $l = vt$, so $\theta = \frac{vt}{r}$

How do we calculate clock hand angles? A clock face is a circle divided into 12 equal parts.

  • Per hour: $360^\circ \div 12 = 30^\circ = \frac{\pi}{6}$ radians
  • Per minute: $360^\circ \div 60 = 6^\circ = \frac{\pi}{30}$ radians
  • Minute hand: $60$ minutes = $360^\circ$, so $1$ minute = $6^\circ$
  • Hour hand: $12$ hours = $360^\circ$, so $1$ hour = $30^\circ$, and $1$ minute = $0.5^\circ$

Example - Clock angle calculation: Find the angle between hour and minute hands at 3:00.

  • Minute hand at 12 (0°), hour hand at 3 (90°)
  • Difference = $90^\circ = \frac{\pi}{2}$ rad

Example - Clock angle at 4:30:

  • Minute hand at 30 minutes = $180^\circ$
  • Hour hand at 4.5 hours = $4.5 \times 30^\circ = 135^\circ$
  • Difference = $180^\circ - 135^\circ = 45^\circ = \frac{\pi}{4}$ rad

Important note: Always convert angles to radians before using $l = r\theta$ or $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.

What is the relationship between areas and linear measurements in similar figures? For any two similar figures:

  • Area Ratio Theorem: $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{L_1}{L_2}\right)^2$
  • The ratio of areas is equal to the square of the ratio of corresponding linear measurements
  • Example: If side lengths are $4$ and $6$, linear ratio = $4:6 = 2:3$, area ratio = $(2/3)^2 = 4:9$ or $16:36$
  • Important: A common mistake is to treat area ratio as equal to linear ratio (that's only true for perimeters, not areas)

How do we prove the area ratio theorem? For similar triangles, area = $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$. Since base and height both scale by factor $k$, area scales by $k^2$.

How do we calculate actual areas from maps? Maps use a linear scale factor $k = \frac{\text{length on map}}{\text{actual length}}$.

  • Actual area = $\frac{\text{map area}}{k^2}$
  • Example: If map scale is $1:1000$, then $k = \frac{1}{1000}$, so $k^2 = \frac{1}{1,000,000}$
  • If map area = $5$ cm², actual area = $5 \times 1,000,000 = 5,000,000$ cm² = $500$ m²
  • Unit conversion: $1$ m² = $10,000$ cm²

What is the area-linear relationship for circles and spheres?

  • For circles: Area $= \pi r^2$. If radii are $r_1$ and $r_2$, then $\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2} = \left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2$
  • Example: Radii $6$ and $8$ → area ratio $\frac{36}{64} = \frac{9}{16}$
  • For spheres: Surface area $= 4\pi r^2$. Same relationship: $\frac{SA_1}{SA_2} = \left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2$

How do we solve for unknown lengths using area ratios? Rearrange the area ratio formula:

  • $\frac{L_2}{L_1} = \sqrt{\frac{A_2}{A_1}}$
  • $L_2 = L_1 \times \sqrt{\frac{A_2}{A_1}}$
  • Example: If $A_1 = 25$ cm², $A_2 = 100$ cm², and $L_1 = 5$ cm, then $L_2 = 5 \times \sqrt{100/25} = 5 \times \sqrt{4} = 5 \times 2 = 10$ cm

How do we calculate the area of a trapezium formed by parallel lines? When a line parallel to the base of a triangle cuts the other two sides, it creates a smaller similar triangle and a trapezium.

  • $\text{Area of trapezium} = \text{Area of larger triangle} - \text{Area of smaller triangle}$
  • Example: In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$, with $AD:DB = 1:2$. Then $AD:AB = 1:3$, so area of $\triangle ADE$ = $(1/3)^2 = 1/9$ of $\triangle ABC$
  • Area of trapezium $BDEC = A_{ABC} - A_{ADE} = A_{ABC} - \frac{1}{9}A_{ABC} = \frac{8}{9}A_{ABC}$

How do surface areas of similar solids compare? For similar three-dimensional figures (cuboids, spheres, cylinders):

  • Surface area ratio = (linear scale factor)$^2$
  • Example: Two similar cuboids with heights in ratio $2:3$ have surface areas in ratio $4:9$
  • Volume ratio = (linear scale factor)$^3$ (for your information, though not the focus of this exercise)

Example - Trapezium area from parallel lines: In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$, $AD = 4$ cm, $DB = 8$ cm, area of $\triangle ADE = 12$ cm². Find area of trapezium $BDEC$.

  • $AB = AD + DB = 4 + 8 = 12$ cm
  • Linear ratio $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}$
  • Area ratio $\frac{A_{ADE}}{A_{ABC}} = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{9}$
  • $A_{ABC} = A_{ADE} \times 9 = 12 \times 9 = 108$ cm²
  • Area of trapezium $BDEC = A_{ABC} - A_{ADE} = 108 - 12 = 96$ cm²

Key formulas summary:

  • Area ratio = (linear ratio)$^2$
  • Linear ratio = $\sqrt{\text{area ratio}}$
  • Map: actual area = map area ÷ $k^2$ (where $k$ is linear scale factor)
  • For trapezium from parallel lines: $A_{\text{trapezium}} = A_{\text{large}\triangle} - A_{\text{small}\triangle}$

What makes two geometric figures similar? Two figures are similar if they have the same shape (not necessarily the same size):

  • All corresponding angles are equal
  • The ratios of the lengths of their corresponding sides are proportional
  • Symbol: $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ (read as "triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF")

What is the AA similarity criterion for triangles? If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, the triangles are similar.

  • Example: Two right-angled triangles with $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ angles are similar
  • Note: AAA (all three angles equal) is equivalent to AA because the third angle is determined by the first two

How do we solve for unknown side lengths using proportion? Use the property that corresponding sides of similar figures are proportional.

  • If $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, then $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{CA}{FD} = k$ (scale factor)
  • Example: $\frac{x}{5} = \frac{8}{4}$ → $x = 5 \times \frac{8}{4} = 5 \times 2 = 10$ cm

How do we verify similarity? Compare corresponding angles and calculate side ratios:

  • Similar figures: All side ratios equal (e.g., $3/2 = 6/4 = 9/6$)
  • Non-similar figures: Side ratios not equal

How do we solve shadow problems using similar triangles? A vertical object and its shadow form a right triangle with the sun's rays. Two different objects at the same time produce similar triangles.

  • Example: A pole of height $h$ casts a shadow of length $s$. A smaller object of height $h_1$ casts shadow $s_1$.
  • Proportion: $\frac{h}{s} = \frac{h_1}{s_1}$
  • Example: If a 6 ft person casts a 4 ft shadow, and a pole casts a 12 ft shadow, then $\frac{6}{4} = \frac{h}{12}$ → $h = 18$ ft

How do we find tree height using line of sight? Using an observer's eye level and distance to the tree, similar triangles can determine the tree's height.

  • Let observer's eye height = $h_e$, distance to tree = $d$, measured angle or proportion

How does similarity apply to lenses (optics)? In convex lenses, similar triangles are formed between the object and its image.

  • Magnification formula: $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = \frac{v}{u}$
  • $h_i$ = image height, $h_o$ = object height
  • $v$ = image distance from lens, $u$ = object distance from lens
  • Example: If $h_o = 5$ cm, $u = 10$ cm, $v = 20$ cm, then $m = 20/10 = 2$, so $h_i = m \times h_o = 2 \times 5 = 10$ cm

How do we handle parallel lines within triangles? A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally.

  • If $DE \parallel BC$ in $\triangle ABC$, then $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$
  • This creates smaller similar triangles: $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$
  • Example: Find $x$ and $y$ using side ratios from parallel lines

Example - Parallel lines in triangle: In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$, $AD = 3$, $DB = 6$, $AE = 4$, find $EC$.

  • $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$ → $\frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{EC}$ → $EC = 8$

How do we solve for unknown quantities in proportional figures? Set up ratios for similar triangles, rectangles, and trapezoids:

  • For similar rectangles: $\frac{\text{length}_1}{\text{length}_2} = \frac{\text{width}_1}{\text{width}_2}$
  • For similar trapezoids: Corresponding side ratios are equal

Important note: Always correctly pair corresponding sides in proportions. The sides used in the numerator must belong to the same figure, and those in the denominator to the other figure.

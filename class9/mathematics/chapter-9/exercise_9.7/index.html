What is a locus in geometry? A locus is a set of all points that satisfy a specific condition or property.

  • Example 1: The locus of points at a fixed distance $r$ from a fixed point $O$ is a circle with center $O$ and radius $r$
  • Example 2: The locus of points equidistant from two fixed points $A$ and $B$ is the perpendicular bisector of segment $AB$
  • Example 3: The locus of points at a fixed distance $d$ from a line $l$ is two parallel lines (one on each side of $l$)

How do we find the distance between two parallel loci? When two loci are drawn parallel to a horizontal line $l$ (one above and one below), the total distance between them is the sum of their individual distances from the line.

  • Example: One locus at $3$ cm above line $l$, another at $3.2$ cm below → distance between them = $3 + 3.2 = 6.2$ cm

What are the points of concurrency in a triangle? Concurrency is when three or more lines intersect at a single point.

  • Circumcenter: Intersection of perpendicular bisectors of sides. Equidistant from all three vertices. Center of circumscribed circle (circumcircle).
  • Incenter: Intersection of angle bisectors. Equidistant from all three sides. Center of inscribed circle (incircle).
  • Centroid: Intersection of medians. Center of mass of triangle.
  • Orthocenter: Intersection of altitudes.

What is the circumcenter? The circumcenter is the unique point equidistant from the three vertices of a triangle. It is found at the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides.

  • For an acute triangle, circumcenter lies inside the triangle
  • For an obtuse triangle, circumcenter lies outside the triangle
  • For a right triangle, circumcenter lies at the midpoint of the hypotenuse

How do we prove properties of bisectors in an isosceles triangle? In an isosceles triangle with $AB = AC$:

  • The angle bisector of $\angle A$ (vertex angle) is also the perpendicular bisector of base $BC$
  • Proof using ASA congruence: Triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ are congruent by ASA ($AB = AC$, $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$, $AD = AD$)
  • Therefore $BD = CD$ ($D$ is midpoint) and $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^\circ$ (perpendicular)

What happens in an equilateral triangle? In an equilateral triangle:

  • All sides are equal, all angles are $60^\circ$
  • The points of intersection for side bisectors and angle bisectors coincide at the same point
  • Circumcenter, incenter, centroid, and orthocenter are all the same point

What is the relationship between bisectors of supplementary angles? Supplementary angles sum to $180^\circ$ (e.g., $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$).

  • The angle bisectors of two intersecting lines (forming supplementary angles) are perpendicular to each other
  • Example: The bisector of $60^\circ$ and the bisector of $120^\circ$ are perpendicular
  • Reason: The angle between the bisectors = $\frac{60^\circ}{2} + \frac{120^\circ}{2} = 30^\circ + 60^\circ = 90^\circ$

How do we name and describe specific loci?

  • Points equidistant from two fixed points: Perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them
  • Points at a fixed distance from a line: A pair of parallel lines (one on each side)
  • Points equidistant from two intersecting lines: Angle bisectors (two lines, perpendicular to each other)
  • Points inside an angle equidistant from the arms: Angle bisector of that angle

Key points to remember for geometric proofs:

  • Provide a logical reason for every statement (e.g., \"corresponding sides of congruent triangles\", \"ASA congruence\", \"definition of perpendicular bisector\")
  • For loci problems, ensure constraints like \"above\" or \"below\" are accurately interpreted
  • For concurrency problems, remember that the circumcenter is found using perpendicular bisectors, not angle bisectors

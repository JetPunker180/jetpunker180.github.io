What is the difference between axioms and conjectures?

  • Axiom: A self-evident truth that is accepted as a starting point without proof. Examples: "Things equal to the same thing are equal to each other", "If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal".
  • Conjecture: A proposition that is suspected to be true based on observed patterns but requires formal proof or disproof.
  • Theorem: A statement that has been proven true based on axioms and previously proven theorems.

How do we identify mathematical statements? A mathematical statement must be a declarative sentence that is either strictly true or strictly false.

  • Example of statement: "The sum of angles in a triangle is $180^\circ$" (True)
  • Example of statement: "$2 + 2 = 5$" (False, but still a statement)
  • Not a statement: "$a + b = 9$" (undetermined until values are assigned)
  • Not a statement: "Close the door" (imperative sentence)

How do we find patterns and make conjectures? Observe the pattern and formulate a general rule.

  • Example: Sequence $1, 3, 7, 15, 31, \dots$
  • Pattern: $1 = 2^1 - 1$, $3 = 2^2 - 1$, $7 = 2^3 - 1$, $15 = 2^4 - 1$, $31 = 2^5 - 1$
  • Conjecture: The $n$th term is $2^n - 1$
  • Next term: $2^6 - 1 = 64 - 1 = 63$

What are common geometric postulates? Postulates are basic assumptions specifically used in geometry.

  • Through any two points, there is exactly one line
  • A line segment can be extended indefinitely in both directions
  • A circle can be drawn with any center and any radius
  • All right angles are equal to each other ($90^\circ$)
  • If a transversal cuts two parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal

What are the properties of equality as axioms?

  • Reflexive Property: $a = a$
  • Symmetric Property: If $a = b$, then $b = a$
  • Transitive Property: If $a = b$ and $b = c$, then $a = c$
  • Addition Property: If $a = b$, then $a + c = b + c$
  • Subtraction Property: If $a = b$, then $a - c = b - c$
  • Multiplication Property: If $a = b$, then $ac = bc$
  • Division Property: If $a = b$ and $c \neq 0$, then $a/c = b/c$
  • Substitution Property: If $a = b$, then $a$ can replace $b$ in any expression

What is the structure of a formal geometric proof? A complete geometric proof has six standard parts:

  • 1. Given: The information provided as a starting point
  • 2. To Prove: The statement that needs to be proven
  • 3. Construction: Any auxiliary lines, points, or figures added to help prove
  • 4. Proof: A logical sequence of statements with corresponding reasons
  • 5. Conclusion: The final statement confirming what was proved
  • 6. Diagram: A visual representation of the geometric figure

What is the difference between theorems and axioms?

  • Axioms/Postulates: Statements accepted without proof. Examples: Symmetric Property of Equality, "Through two points there is exactly one line".
  • Theorems: Statements that require proof. Examples: Isosceles Triangle Theorem, Pythagorean Theorem.
  • Distinguishing: Axioms are the foundation; theorems are built upon axioms using logical reasoning.

Important note: In geometric proofs, every statement in the "Proof" section must have a corresponding reason (axiom, postulate, theorem, or property).

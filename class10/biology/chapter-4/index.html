What is the urinary system? The urinary system (excretory system) is responsible for removing waste products from the blood, regulating water and electrolyte balance (osmoregulation), and maintaining blood pH. It consists of a pair of kidneys, a pair of ureters, a urinary bladder, and a urethra.

What is the functional unit of the kidney? The nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney. Each human kidney contains approximately 1 million nephrons ($1 \times 10^6$). Nephrons are responsible for filtering blood and producing urine.

What are the parts of a nephron? A nephron is divided into two main parts:

  • Renal Corpuscle:
    • Glomerulus: A tuft of capillaries where blood filtration begins.
    • Bowman''s Capsule: Cup-shaped structure surrounding the glomerulus that collects filtrate.
  • Renal Tubule:
    • Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT): Site of selective reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, and ions.
    • Loop of Henle: U-shaped portion that establishes concentration gradient for water reabsorption.
    • Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT): Site of tubular secretion and fine regulation of ions.
    • Collecting Duct: Collects urine from multiple nephrons and carries it to the renal pelvis.

What is the process of urine formation? Urine formation involves three main processes:

  1. Pressure Filtration (Glomerular Filtration): Blood pressure forces water and small solutes (glucose, amino acids, urea, ions) from the glomerulus into Bowman''s capsule. Large molecules like proteins and blood cells remain in blood.
  2. Selective Reabsorption: Essential substances (glucose, amino acids, 99% of water, and ions) are reabsorbed from the renal tubule back into the bloodstream via osmosis and active transport.
  3. Tubular Secretion: Additional waste products ($H^+$, $K^+$, drugs, toxins) are actively secreted from blood into the tubule for excretion.

What is osmoregulation? Osmoregulation is the process by which the kidneys regulate the concentration of water and salts in the blood to maintain homeostasis. The kidneys adjust urine concentration based on the body''s hydration levels:

  • Dehydration: Kidneys produce concentrated urine (less water, more solutes) to conserve water.
  • Overhydration: Kidneys produce dilute urine (more water, less solutes) to excrete excess water.

What is the composition of urine? Normal urine composition:

  • 95% water
  • Urea (9.3 g/L)
  • Uric acid
  • Creatinine
  • Ions (sodium, potassium, chloride)

What are kidney stones and how are they treated? Kidney stones (renal calculi) are hard deposits of minerals and salts that form in the kidneys.

  • Treatment:
    • Small stones: Drink plenty of water to pass naturally.
    • Larger stones: Lithotripsy (shock wave therapy) to break stones into smaller pieces.
    • Surgery: Surgical removal for very large stones.

What is dialysis? Dialysis is a medical treatment that performs the functions of the kidneys when they fail.

  • Haemodialysis: Blood is pumped out of the body into a dialyzer machine (artificial kidney) where it is filtered and then returned to the body.
  • Peritoneal Dialysis: A catheter is placed in the abdominal cavity, and dialysate fluid is introduced. The peritoneum (membrane lining the abdomen) acts as a natural filter.

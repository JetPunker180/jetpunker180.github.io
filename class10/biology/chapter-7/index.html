What is inheritance? Inheritance is the transmission of genetic information from parents to offspring. It explains how traits are passed from one generation to the next.

What is the basic organization of a chromosome? Chromosomes are thread-like structures made of DNA and proteins. During division, they compact into thick chromatids to prevent damage.

  • Nucleosome: Consists of DNA wrapped around eight histone proteins (like beads on a string).
  • Sister chromatids: Two identical copies of a chromosome attached at the centromere.
  • Centromere: Constricted region where sister chromatids are attached.
  • Telomeres: Protective caps at the ends of chromosomes to prevent degradation of genetic information.

How do Genotype and Phenotype differ?

  • Genotype: The specific genetic makeup of an individual (e.g., RR or Rr).
  • Phenotype: The observable physical trait expressed (e.g., round seeds).
  • Alleles: Different forms of a gene located at the same locus on homologous chromosomes.

What is Mendel''s Law of Segregation? During gamete formation, the two alleles for a trait segregate (separate) so each gamete receives only one allele. The pair is restored when gametes are fertilized.

What is Mendel''s Law of Independent Assortment? Alleles for different traits assort independently during gamete formation. This explains the 9:3:3:1 ratio in dihybrid crosses.

What are the results of a monohybrid cross? A monohybrid cross involves one trait:

  • Phenotypic ratio: 3:1 (dominant : recessive)
  • Genotypic ratio: 1:2:1 (RR : Rr : rr)

What are the results of a dihybrid cross? A dihybrid cross involves two traits (e.g., seed shape and color) and results in a phenotypic ratio of 9:3:3:1, demonstrating independent assortment.

What is a test cross? A test cross is used to determine an unknown genotype. By crossing an organism showing a dominant trait with a homozygous recessive (rr) individual, we can identify if the parent is homozygous dominant (RR) or heterozygous (Rr) based on the offspring''s phenotypes.

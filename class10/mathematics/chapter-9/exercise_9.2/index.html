How do we determine the absolute radial dimensions of a circle when multiple parallel chords establish nested horizontal segments along a single side of the center point? Moving deeper into circles requires solving systems of equations where geometric theorems generate algebraic variables.

Isosceles Radial Triangle Structures: Any sector bounded by two radii and a chord forms a natural isosceles triangle ($\triangle OCF$ with $OC = OF = r$). Opposite interior angles are equal: $\angle OCF = \angle OFC$.

Example: Central angle $\angle COF = 150^\circ$:

  • $2 \times \angle OCF + 150^\circ = 180^\circ$
  • $2 \times \angle OCF = 30^\circ$ → $\angle OCF = 15^\circ$

Dual-System Pythagorean Chord Calculations: When two unequal parallel chords rest on the same side of a circle's center, a perpendicular from center $O$ bisects both chords, creating two separate right triangles sharing the same radius $r$.

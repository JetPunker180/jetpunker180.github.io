How do the fundamental geometric lines inside a circle intersect to define unique radial paths and perpendicular balance across structural chords? Circle geometry expands beyond basic perimeter boundaries by analyzing how internal line segments interact with the true center point.

Uniqueness of a Circumcircle: Given three non-collinear points (e.g., vertices of a triangle), there exists exactly one circle passing through all three points. The circumcenter ($O$) is located at the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the triangle's sides.

Perpendicular Bisector Property: A line drawn from the center of a circle to bisect a chord (that is not a diameter) meets that chord at a right angle ($90^\circ$). Conversely, a perpendicular from the center bisects the chord.

Key Theorem: A diameter that bisects a non-diameter chord is perpendicular to that chord.

Right-Triangle Verification of Chord Distance: Using the Pythagorean theorem to verify perpendicularity.

Example: Circle with diameter $10$ cm ($OA = 5$ cm), chord $AB = 8$ cm ($AE = 4$ cm), $OE = 3$ cm:

  • Apply Pythagorean theorem: $OA^2 = OE^2 + AE^2$
  • $5^2 = 3^2 + 4^2$ → $25 = 9 + 16$ → $25 = 25$ ✓
  • Since the balance holds, $\angle OEA = 90^\circ$, proving the diameter cuts the chord perpendicularly.

Important Rule: A line from the center only creates a $90^\circ$ angle if it passes through the exact midpoint of the chord (bisecting it).

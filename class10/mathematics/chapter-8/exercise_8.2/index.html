How do we apply right-angled ratios to solve unknown structural metrics and calculate distance tracks from real-world vantage points? Right-triangle trigonometry utilizes ratios of sides to calculate unmeasurable physical spaces.

Solving Complete Right-Angled Triangles: Fully solving a right triangle ($\gamma = 90^\circ$) requires establishing three side parameters ($a, b, c$) and three angular bounds ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$).

Example: Given hypotenuse $c = 15$ m and angle $\alpha = 40^\circ$:

  • Complementary angle: $\beta = 180^\circ - (90^\circ + 40^\circ) = 50^\circ$
  • $a = 15 \times \sin 40^\circ \approx 9.64$ m
  • $b = 15 \times \cos 40^\circ \approx 11.49$ m

Angles of Elevation and Depression:

  • Angle of Elevation: Looking upward from horizontal line of sight
  • Angle of Depression: Looking downward from horizontal line of sight (measured from horizontal at observer point)
  • By alternate interior angle geometry, angle of depression from top equals angle of elevation from bottom

Shoreline Distance Example: Cliff height $100$ m, angle of depression $7^\circ$:

  • $\tan 7^\circ = \frac{100}{d}$
  • $d = \frac{100}{\tan 7^\circ} \approx \frac{100}{0.12278} \approx 814.3$ m

Multi-Object Differential Heights: Observer at midpoint between two landmarks ($50$ m to each), angles $13^\circ$ and $18^\circ$:

  • $h_1 = 50 \times \tan 13^\circ$
  • $h_2 = 50 \times \tan 18^\circ$
  • Height difference = $h_2 - h_1$

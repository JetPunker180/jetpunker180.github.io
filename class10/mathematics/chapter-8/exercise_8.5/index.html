How do we connect the internal angular geometry of triangles with the boundary dimensions of their inscribed, circumscribed, and escaped circular structures? The final section of Chapter 8 unifies oblique triangle properties with advanced circle measurements, bridging the gap between pure algebra and coordinate geometry.

Inradius ($r$) Formula: The radius of an internal circle tangent to all three sides.

  • $r = \frac{\Delta}{s}$

Example: $a=10, b=13, c=17$

  • $s = \frac{10+13+17}{2} = 20$
  • $\Delta = \sqrt{20(10)(7)(3)} = 10\sqrt{42}$
  • $r = \frac{10\sqrt{42}}{20} = \frac{\sqrt{42}}{2} \approx 3.24$ units

Circumradius ($R$) Formula: The radius of an external circle passing through all three vertices.

  • $R = \frac{abc}{4\Delta}$

Example: $R = \frac{10 \times 13 \times 17}{4(10\sqrt{42})} = \frac{2210}{40\sqrt{42}} = \frac{221}{4\sqrt{42}} \approx 8.53$ units

Exradii ($r_1, r_2, r_3$) Formulas: Radii of excircles tangent to one side externally.

  • $r_1 = \frac{\Delta}{s-a}$, $r_2 = \frac{\Delta}{s-b}$, $r_3 = \frac{\Delta}{s-c}$

Example: $r_1 = \frac{10\sqrt{42}}{20-10} = \sqrt{42} \approx 6.48$ units

Half-Angle Identities:

  • $\tan(\frac{\alpha}{2}) = \sqrt{\frac{(s-b)(s-c)}{s(s-a)}}$
  • $\tan(\frac{\beta}{2}) = \sqrt{\frac{(s-a)(s-c)}{s(s-b)}}$
  • $\tan(\frac{\gamma}{2}) = \sqrt{\frac{(s-a)(s-b)}{s(s-c)}}$

Proof of $r = s\tan(\frac{\alpha}{2})\tan(\frac{\beta}{2})\tan(\frac{\gamma}{2})$:

  • Multiply the three half-angle tangent formulas inside a radical
  • Terms cancel to leave $r = \Delta/s$

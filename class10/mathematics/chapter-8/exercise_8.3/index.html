How do we compute missing angles and dimensions when a triangle does not contain a right angle? While basic right-triangle trigonometric functions rely on absolute horizontal-vertical baselines, oblique triangles require general mathematical laws that balance dimensions regardless of internal angle shapes.

The Law of Cosines (SAS Configuration): For two known sides ($a$, $b$) and their included angle ($\gamma$):

  • $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos\gamma$

Example: $a = 7$, $b = 12$, $\gamma = 58.5^\circ$

  • $c^2 = 7^2 + 12^2 - 2(7)(12)\cos58.5^\circ = 49 + 144 - 168(0.5225)$
  • $c^2 \approx 193 - 87.78 = 105.22$ → $c \approx 10.2$ units

The Law of Sines (AAS/ASA Configuration):

  • $\frac{a}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{b}{\sin\beta} = \frac{c}{\sin\gamma}$

Example: $a = 10$, $\alpha = 40^\circ$, $\beta = 60^\circ$

  • $b = \frac{10 \times \sin60^\circ}{\sin40^\circ} \approx \frac{10 \times 0.8660}{0.6428} \approx 13.5$ units

Interior Angle Sum Rule:

  • $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ$
  • Example: $\alpha = 50^\circ$, $\beta = 70^\circ$ → $\gamma = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$

Important Rule: Use Law of Cosines for SAS (two sides, included angle). Use Law of Sines for AAS/ASA (two angles, one side).

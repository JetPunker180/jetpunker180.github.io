What are the key theoretical properties of complex conjugates? For a complex number $z = x + iy$, its complex conjugate is $\overline{z} = x - iy$. Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate satisfies: $z\overline{z} = (x + iy)(x - iy) = x^2 - (iy)^2 = x^2 - (-y^2) = x^2 + y^2$, which always evaluates to a non-negative real number.

How do we simplify higher powers of iota? Iota ($i$) has a cyclic period of 4: $i^1 = i$, $i^2 = -1$, $i^3 = -i$, $i^4 = 1$, and then repeats. For higher powers, divide the exponent by 4: $i^{4n} = 1$, $i^{4n+1} = i$, $i^{4n+2} = -1$, $i^{4n+3} = -i$. Example: $1 + (-i)^9 = 1 - i^9 = 1 - (i^4)^2 \cdot i = 1 - i$.

How do we verify purely imaginary conjugate roots? To verify that $x = \pm\sqrt{5}i$ are roots of $x^2 + 5 = 0$, substitute: $(\pm\sqrt{5}i)^2 + 5 = 5i^2 + 5 = 5(-1) + 5 = -5 + 5 = 0$.

How are complex numbers interpreted geometrically as vectors? Complex numbers on an Argand plane can be treated as directional geometric vectors.

Head-to-Tail Method for vector addition: To add multiple complex numbers, place the starting point (tail) of the subsequent vector directly at the terminal point (head) of the preceding vector. The resultant vector is drawn straight from the initial origin to the terminal endpoint of the final vector.

Parallelogram Method for vector addition: For two complex vectors, align them tail-to-tail to form adjacent sides of a parallelogram. The diagonal originating from their common vertex represents the direct mathematical sum of the two numbers.

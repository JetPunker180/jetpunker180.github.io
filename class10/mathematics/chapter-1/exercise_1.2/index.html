How do we determine the additive inverse of a complex number? For any complex number $z = a + bi$, its additive inverse is the unique number that when added to $z$ gives $0 + 0i$. Equating real and imaginary components gives $-z = -a - bi$. Example: The additive inverse of $-4+5i$ is $4-5i$; the additive inverse of $-3-3i$ is $3+3i$.

How do we determine the multiplicative inverse of a complex number? The multiplicative inverse (reciprocal) of $z = a + bi$ is $z^{-1} = \frac{1}{a+bi} = \frac{a - bi}{a^2 + b^2} = \frac{\bar{z}}{|z|^2}$. This is obtained by multiplying numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate.

What is the complex plane (Argand diagram)? Complex numbers can be plotted as ordered pairs on a two-dimensional coordinate system called the complex plane or Argand diagram. The horizontal axis (x-axis) represents the real part, while the vertical axis (y-axis) represents the imaginary part.

How do we convert complex numbers to coordinates? A complex number $z = a + bi$ maps directly to the Cartesian coordinates $(a, b)$. For example:

  • $z = -2 + 4i$ maps to $(-2, 4)$ — second quadrant
  • $z = 3 - i$ maps to $(3, -1)$ — fourth quadrant
  • $z = -3 - 3i$ maps to $(-3, -3)$ — third quadrant
  • $z = 2 + 5i$ maps to $(2, 5)$ — first quadrant

How do we graphically represent complex quotients? When evaluating complex fractional operations, first simplify algebraically, then plot the result. Example: $z_1 = 6-4i$, $z_2 = 3$: $\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{6}{3} - \frac{4}{3}i = 2 - \frac{4}{3}i$. This evaluates to coordinates $(2, -1.33)$, which is plotted in the fourth quadrant.

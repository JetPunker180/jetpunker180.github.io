What defines an imaginary number and how is it simplified from a negative radical? An imaginary number is defined using the fundamental imaginary unit iota, where $i = \sqrt{-1}$ and $i^2 = -1$. When simplifying a square root containing a negative radicand, the negative factor is separated out as a multiple of $-1$. For example: $\sqrt{-3} = \sqrt{3 \times (-1)} = \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{-1} = \sqrt{3}i$. Similarly, $\sqrt{-\frac{4}{9}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{9} \times (-1)} = \frac{2}{3}i$.

What are the powers of iota? The powers of i follow a repeating cycle every 4:

  • $i^1 = i$
  • $i^2 = -1$
  • $i^3 = i^2 \cdot i = -i$
  • $i^4 = (i^2)^2 = (-1)^2 = 1$
  • For higher powers, divide exponent by 4: $i^{4n} = 1$, $i^{4n+1} = i$, $i^{4n+2} = -1$, $i^{4n+3} = -i$

What is the standard form of a complex number? A complex number is written in the form $a + bi$, where $a$ is the real part and $b$ is the imaginary part. The complex conjugate of $a + bi$ is $a - bi$.

How do we rationalize complex denominators? To simplify an expression where $i$ is in the denominator, multiply both numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate of the denominator: $(a - bi)(a + bi) = a^2 - (bi)^2 = a^2 + b^2$.

Example - Binomial expansion: $(1-i)^2 = 1 - 2i + i^2 = 1 - 2i - 1 = -2i$.

Example - Rationalization: $\frac{1}{1+i} = \frac{1}{1+i} \times \frac{1-i}{1-i} = \frac{1-i}{1^2 + 1^2} = \frac{1-i}{2} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}i$.

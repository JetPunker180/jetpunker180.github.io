How do we perform arithmetic operations on rational algebraic expressions? Operating on rational expressions involves the same fundamental principles as operating on numerical fractions. To add or subtract rational expressions, we must first establish a least common denominator (LCD).

Simple Example: $\frac{x}{2} + \frac{x}{5}$ → LCD of 2 and 5 is 10 → $\frac{5x}{10} + \frac{2x}{10} = \frac{7x}{10}$

Advanced Operations with Factoring Denominators:

  • Step 1: Factor Denominators
    • $5+x-18x^2 = (5-9x)(2x+1)$
    • $2+5x+2x^2 = (2x+1)(x+2)$
  • Step 2: Construct LCD → $(5-9x)(2x+1)(x+2)$
  • Step 3: Balance Numerators → Multiply each numerator by missing factors from its original denominator
  • Step 4: Combine Like Terms → Expand, add/subtract, simplify

Multiplication and Division:

  • Multiplication: Multiply numerators together, denominators together, then simplify
  • Division: Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor fraction
  • Cross-canceling: Factor all components to facilitate cancellation

Sign Distribution: When a minus sign sits before a fraction (e.g., $-\frac{m+1}{24m}$), distribute the negative across every term inside the numerator: $-\frac{m+1}{24m} = \frac{-m-1}{24m}$

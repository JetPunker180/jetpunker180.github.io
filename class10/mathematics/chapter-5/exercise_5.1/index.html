How do we simplify rational algebraic expressions to their lowest terms? A rational expression is written in the form $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$, where $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$ are polynomials and $Q(x) \neq 0$.

Example: Simplify $\frac{15ax^3y^2}{25a^2xy^6}$

  1. Divide numerical coefficients: $\frac{15}{25} = \frac{3}{5}$
  2. Apply exponent laws: $a^{1-2} = a^{-1}$, $x^{3-1} = x^2$, $y^{2-6} = y^{-4}$
  3. Rearrange negative powers to denominator: $\frac{3x^2}{5ay^4}$

Algebraic Functional Evaluation:

Given $g(x) = \frac{1}{2x^3} + \frac{x}{2} + 2$, find $g(-\frac{1}{3})$:

  1. Cube the fraction: $(-\frac{1}{3})^3 = -\frac{1}{27}$
  2. Compute $\frac{1}{2x^3} = \frac{1}{2(-1/27)} = \frac{1}{-2/27} = -\frac{27}{2}$
  3. Compute $\frac{x}{2} = \frac{-1/3}{2} = -\frac{1}{6}$
  4. Combine: $-\frac{27}{2} - \frac{1}{6} + 2$
  5. Find LCD (6): $-\frac{81}{6} - \frac{1}{6} + \frac{12}{6} = -\frac{70}{6} = -\frac{35}{3}$

Verifying Equivalent Identities: Calculate $P(2)$, $Q(2)$, and $D(2)$ separately to confirm $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = D(x)$ at specific points.

Domain Constraints: A rational expression is undefined if any substitution forces $Q(x) = 0$.

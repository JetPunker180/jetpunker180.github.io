How do we solve linear inequalities and represent them graphically? Linear inequalities are solved similarly to linear equations, with one crucial exception: if you multiply or divide by a negative number, the direction of the inequality symbol must be reversed.

Example: $5x - 12 \le 3x - 4$

  1. Subtract $3x$ from both sides: $2x - 12 \le -4$
  2. Add $12$ to both sides: $2x \le 8$
  3. Divide by $2$: $x \le 4$

Number Line Representation:

  • Closed circle (●) at $x=4$ indicates the boundary value is included ($\le$ or $\ge$)
  • Open circle (○) indicates the boundary is excluded ($<$ or $>$)
  • Shading to the left indicates values less than the boundary; shading to the right indicates greater than

Practical Constraints - Garden Perimeter Problem:

A rectangular garden has width $w = 2l$ (twice the length). Maximum perimeter $P \le 60$ meters:

  • $P = 2l + 2w = 2l + 2(2l) = 2l + 4l = 6l$
  • $6l \le 60$ → $l \le 10$ meters
  • Domain restriction: $l > 0$ (length must be positive)
  • Solution: $0 < l \le 10$ meters

Inequality Reversal Rule Examples:

  • $-2x < 6$ → divide by $-2$, flip $<$ to $>$ → $x > -3$
  • $-4x \ge 12$ → divide by $-4$, flip $\ge$ to $\le$ → $x \le -3$

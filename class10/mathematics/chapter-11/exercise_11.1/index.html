How do we translate geometric axioms into precise physical constructions using only an unmarked straightedge and a compass, uncovering hidden central centers and drawing perfect tangent vectors? Practical geometry turns abstract theorems into physical blueprints.

Locating the Center of a Circle or Arc: Using perpendicular bisectors of two non-parallel chords:

  1. Draw two non-parallel chords $AB$ and $PQ$ across the circular region
  2. For chord $AB$: center compass at $A$, radius > half of $AB$, swing arcs both sides; repeat from $B$ → intersection points give perpendicular bisector $DE$
  3. Repeat for chord $PQ$ → perpendicular bisector $LM$
  4. Intersection of $DE$ and $LM$ = center point $O$

Tangent Construction from Point on Circumference:

  1. Draw radius $\overline{OP}$ from center to point $P$
  2. Construct $90^\circ$ angle at point $P$ (tangent $\perp$ radius)
  3. Extend to form tangent line $PQ$

Tangent Construction from External Point:

  1. Join center $O$ to external point $P$
  2. Construct perpendicular bisector of $OP$ → midpoint $Q$
  3. Draw helper circle centered at $Q$ with radius $OQ$
  4. Intersection points $A$ and $B$ with original circle = tangency points
  5. Draw $PA$ and $PB$ → two equal tangent lines

Important Exam Note: Always write clear Steps of Construction alongside your drawing.

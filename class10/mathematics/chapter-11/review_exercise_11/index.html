How do you calculate the distance between centers of two touching circles? The relative positioning of two circles determines the mathematical relationship between their centers and the point of contact. This is a frequently tested concept in FBISE multiple-choice questions.

External Contact (Circles touching externally): When two circles with radii $r_1$ and $r_2$ touch externally, their centers and point of contact form a straight, collinear line. The distance between their centers equals the sum of their radii:

  • Formula: $d = r_1 + r_2$
  • Example: If $r_1 = 3$ cm and $r_2 = 3.2$ cm, then $d = 3 + 3.2 = 6.2$ cm

Internal Contact (Circles touching internally): When one circle nests inside another and they touch internally, the distance between centers equals the difference between their radii:

  • Formula: $d = r_1 - r_2$ (where $r_1 > r_2$)
  • Example: If $r_1 = 4.8$ cm and $r_2 = 4$ cm, then $d = 4.8 - 4 = 0.8$ cm

How many common tangents can be drawn to two circles? The number of common tangents depends on the circles' relative positions:

  • Two intersecting circles: Exactly 2 common tangents
  • Externally touching circles: Exactly 3 common tangents
  • Internally touching circles: Exactly 1 common tangent
  • Non-overlapping circles: Exactly 4 common tangents

What is the difference between transverse and direct common tangents?

  • Transverse (Internal) Common Tangents: These cross over the line segment connecting the centers and intersect each other at exactly 1 point (the internal center of similitude).
  • Direct (External) Common Tangents: These do not cross the center line. For two identical (congruent) circles, the direct common tangents run perfectly parallel to each other.

How do you find the radius of a circle inscribed in a square? When a circle is tightly packed inside a square, the circle's outer edge touches all four sides of the square. This makes the circle's full diameter exactly equal to the square's side length.

  • Formula: $d = s$ and $r = \frac{s}{2}$
  • Example: Square side $s = 6$ cm → circle radius $r = 6/2 = 3$ cm

How do you find the side length of a square inscribed in a circle? When a square is drawn inside a circle with all four corners touching the circle's perimeter, the square's diagonal equals the circle's diameter.

  • Formula: $\text{Diagonal} = 2r$ and side length $s = \frac{2r}{\sqrt{2}} = r\sqrt{2}$
  • Example: Circle radius $r = 5$ cm → diagonal = $10$ cm → side length = $10/\sqrt{2} \approx 7.07$ cm

What is the relationship between in-center and circumcenter in an equilateral triangle? Inside any triangle, different lines (angle bisectors, medians, perpendicular bisectors) meet at different points. However, in a perfectly symmetrical equilateral triangle, all these centers coincide at the exact same point. The in-center (intersection of angle bisectors), circumcenter (intersection of perpendicular bisectors), and centroid (intersection of medians) all occupy the same position.

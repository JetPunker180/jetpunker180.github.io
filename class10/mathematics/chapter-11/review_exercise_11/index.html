What defines practical geometry of circles? Practical geometry of circles involves constructing and analyzing geometric figures using compass, straightedge, and mathematical relationships. Miscellaneous Exercise 11 serves as the comprehensive review framework bringing together all key concepts including dual-circle tracking, external and internal contact configurations, common tangents, inscribed polygons, and geometric center properties for FBISE 2026 board examination preparation.

How do we calculate distances between circle centers? The relative positioning of two circles determines the mathematical relationship between their centers and tangents:

  • External Contact: When two circles with radii $r_1$ and $r_2$ touch externally, their centers and point of contact form a straight collinear line. The distance between centers equals the direct sum of their radii: $\text{Distance} = r_1 + r_2$. For example, with $r_1 = 3\text{ cm}$ and $r_2 = 3.2\text{ cm}$, the center distance is $3 + 3.2 = 6.2\text{ cm}$.
  • Internal Contact: When one circle nests inside another and they touch internally, the distance between centers equals the difference between their radii: $\text{Distance} = r_1 - r_2$ (where $r_1 > r_2$). For example, with $r_1 = 4.8\text{ cm}$ and $r_2 = 4\text{ cm}$, the center distance is $4.8 - 4 = 0.8\text{ cm}$.
  • Intersecting Circles: For two circles that intersect at two distinct points, exactly 2 common tangents can be drawn across their combined boundaries.
  • Non-Intersecting Circles: For two circles that neither touch nor intersect, 4 common tangents exist (2 direct and 2 transverse).

What are common tangents and their properties?

  • Direct Common Tangents: These tangents do not cross the line segment joining the centers. For two congruent circles, direct common tangents are parallel to each other.
  • Transverse Common Tangents: These tangents cross the line segment joining the centers and intersect each other at exactly one point between the circles.
  • Tangent Count Summary:
    • Externally touching circles: 3 common tangents (2 direct, 1 transverse at contact point)
    • Internally touching circles: 1 common tangent (at the contact point)
    • Intersecting circles: 2 common tangents (both direct)
    • Non-intersecting circles: 4 common tangents (2 direct, 2 transverse)

How do inscribed and circumscribed polygons relate to circles? Fitting squares and circles inside one another establishes fixed geometric ratios:

  • Circle Inscribed in a Square: When a circle is packed tightly inside a square of side length $s$, the circle touches all four sides. The circle''s diameter $d$ equals the square''s side length ($d = s$), and the radius is $r = \frac{s}{2}$. For a square with $s = 6\text{ cm}$, the circle''s radius is $r = \frac{6}{2} = 3\text{ cm}$.
  • Square Inscribed in a Circle: When a square is drawn inside a circle of radius $r$, all four vertices touch the circle''s circumference. The square''s diagonal $D$ equals the circle''s diameter ($D = 2r$). For a circle with $r = 5\text{ cm}$, the square''s diagonal is $D = 2 \times 5 = 10\text{ cm}$.
  • Circle Circumscribed Around a Square: The circle''s diameter equals the square''s diagonal, so $d_{\text{circle}} = s\sqrt{2}$ and $r_{\text{circle}} = \frac{s\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

What are the center properties of equilateral triangles? Inside a standard triangle, different center points exist:

  • Incenter: Intersection of angle bisectors (center of inscribed circle)
  • Circumcenter: Intersection of perpendicular bisectors (center of circumscribed circle)
  • Centroid: Intersection of medians (center of mass)
  • Orthocenter: Intersection of altitudes

In a perfectly symmetrical equilateral triangle, all four centers coincide at the exact same point. This unique property makes equilateral triangles special in geometric constructions.

How do we solve practical geometry word problems? Practical geometry word problems typically involve:

  • Finding unknown radii or distances: Use contact formulas ($r_1 + r_2$ or $r_1 - r_2$) to find center distances.
  • Determining tangent counts: Analyze whether circles touch, intersect, or are separate.
  • Calculating dimensions of inscribed shapes: Apply diameter = diagonal for squares, diameter = side for circles in squares.

What are the key construction techniques reviewed?

  • Bisecting arcs: Using chord perpendicular bisector method to find arc midpoints.
  • Constructing tangents without centers: Drawing tangents through arc midpoints or endpoints using parallel chord technique.
  • Drawing common tangents: Using geometric construction methods for direct and transverse tangents between two circles.

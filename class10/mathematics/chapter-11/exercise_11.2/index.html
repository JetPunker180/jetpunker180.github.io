How do you bisect an arc without knowing its center using only a compass and straightedge? Arc bisection is a fundamental construction technique in practical geometry. To partition any given arc $\widehat{ABC}$ into two equal segments ($m\widehat{AB} = m\widehat{BC}$) without needing the circle's center:

  1. Draw chord $\overline{AC}$ connecting the endpoints of the arc.
  2. Construct the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{AC}$ by swinging intersecting arcs from points $A$ and $C$.
  3. The point where this bisector intersects the arc marks the exact midpoint $B$.
  4. The two resulting arcs $\widehat{AB}$ and $\widehat{BC}$ are perfectly equal.

How do you construct a tangent to an arc when the center is missing or inaccessible? Drawing a tangent line through a point on an arc without knowing the center requires different approaches depending on the point's position:

Case 1: Tangent through the arc's midpoint:

  • Draw chord $\overline{PA}$ connecting the arc's endpoints.
  • Construct the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{PA}$ to locate midpoint $F$ on the arc.
  • Using a set square or parallel ruler, draw line $\overleftrightarrow{XY}$ through $F$ parallel to chord $\overline{PA}$.
  • Line $\overleftrightarrow{XY}$ is the required tangent at the midpoint.

Case 2: Tangent through the arc's endpoint:

  • Mark two points $P$ and $Q$ on the arc.
  • Draw chords $\overline{AP}$ and $\overline{AQ}$ from the endpoint.
  • Construct perpendiculars to these chords at points $P$ and $Q$.
  • The intersection of these perpendiculars gives the tangent direction at endpoint $A$.

How do you calculate the total length of a belt wrapped around two identical pulleys? This is a classic applied geometry problem testing both linear and circular measurements.

Example problem: Two identical circular pulleys, each with radius $r = 6$ cm, are placed with centers $12$ cm apart. A belt wraps tightly around both pulleys.

Step 1: Straight parallel segments

  • The top and bottom belt segments run parallel to the line joining centers.
  • Each straight segment equals the center-to-center distance $d = 12$ cm.
  • Total straight length = $2 \times 12 = 24$ cm.

Step 2: Curved arc wrapping segments

  • The belt wraps around half of each pulley.
  • Combined, these form one complete circle of radius $r = 6$ cm.
  • Curved length = $2\pi r = 2\pi(6) = 12\pi \approx 37.68$ cm.

Step 3: Total belt length

  • Total = Straight segments + Curved segments
  • Total = $24 + 12\pi \approx 24 + 37.68 = 61.68$ cm.

Formula summary: For two identical pulleys radius $r$ with center distance $d$: $\text{Belt Length} = 2d + 2\pi r$.

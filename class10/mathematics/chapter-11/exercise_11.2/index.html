What defines practical geometry of circles? Practical geometry of circles involves constructing geometric figures using only a compass, straightedge, and ruler without relying on measurements. Exercise 11.2 focuses on advanced circle geometry techniques including arc bisection through chord perpendicular bisectors, tangent construction without center points, and real-world applications in mechanical engineering systems like pulley belt calculations and tensioned wire configurations.

How do we bisect an arc using chord method? To bisect any given arc $\widehat{ABC}$ into two equal parts, we draw chord $\overline{AC}$ connecting endpoints, then construct the perpendicular bisector of chord $\overline{AC}$ using compass arcs. The intersection point $B$ where this bisector meets the arc divides it into two equal arcs: $m\widehat{AB} = m\widehat{BC}$.

How do we construct tangents without center point? There are two cases for constructing tangents to an arc without knowing its center:

  • Case 1: Point at Arc''s Midpoint: Draw chord $\overline{PA}$ connecting endpoints. Construct perpendicular bisector through midpoint $F$. Draw line parallel to chord through $F$ - this is the required tangent.
  • Case 2: Point at Arc''s Endpoint: Use helper points along the arc to generate inscribed angles that guide tangent placement without referencing the center.

What are mechanical pulley belt length calculations? For two identical pulleys of radius $r$ with center distance $d$, the total belt length equals the sum of two straight segments ($2d$) plus two semicircular arcs forming one complete circle ($2\pi r$):

  • Straight Segments: Each straight segment equals center distance $d$. Total = $2d$.
  • Curved Segments: Two semicircular wraps combine to form full circle circumference $2\pi r$.
  • Total Belt Length: $L = 2d + 2\pi r$

What are the key principles of centerless tangent construction? Centerless tangent construction relies on three fundamental geometric principles:

  • Perpendicular Bisector Theorem: The perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the circle''s center, allowing chord-based arc location.
  • Parallel Tangent Theorem: Tangents drawn at the midpoint of an arc are parallel to the chord connecting the arc''s endpoints.
  • Inscribed Angle Theorem: Angles inscribed in the same arc are equal, enabling tangent placement using reference points.

How do we calculate tensioned wire configurations? For a wire stretched between two anchor points creating a right triangle tangent offset, we apply the Pythagorean theorem to find wire length and tangent offsets:

  • Wire Length: $L = \sqrt{d^2 + (r_1 - r_2)^2}$ where $d$ is center distance and $r_1, r_2$ are pulley radii.
  • Tangent Offset: The perpendicular distance from center to tangent line equals the pulley radius.

What is the step-by-step construction process for arc bisection?

  • Step 1: Identify the arc $\widehat{ABC}$ to be bisected with endpoints $A$ and $C$.
  • Step 2: Draw chord $\overline{AC}$ connecting endpoints.
  • Step 3: Using compass, draw arcs from $A$ with radius greater than half of $AC$.
  • Step 4: From $C$, draw intersecting arcs with same radius to create points $P$ and $Q$.
  • Step 5: Draw line through $P$ and $Q$ - this is the perpendicular bisector of chord $AC$.
  • Step 6: The point $B$ where this line intersects the arc is the exact midpoint, creating $m\widehat{AB} = m\widehat{BC}$.

What are the step-by-step instructions for tangent construction through midpoint?

  • Step 1: Locate arc $\widehat{PA}$ with endpoints $P$ and $A$.
  • Step 2: Draw chord $\overline{PA}$ connecting endpoints.
  • Step 3: Construct perpendicular bisector of chord $\overline{PA}$ to find midpoint $F$ on the arc.
  • Step 4: Using set square, draw line through $F$ parallel to chord $\overline{PA}$.
  • Step 5: The resulting line $\overleftrightarrow{XY}$ is the required tangent at midpoint $F$.

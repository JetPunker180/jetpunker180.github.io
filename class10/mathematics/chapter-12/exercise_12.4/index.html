What defines basic statistics? Basic statistics encompasses the collection, organization, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of data. Exercise 12.4 concludes the statistics chapter by introducing modern probability theory, including sample space possibility diagrams, probability tree diagrams, independent event identification, and the product rule for compound events - essential concepts for FBISE 2026 board examination success.

What are sample space possibility diagrams? When an experiment involves two sequential or simultaneous stages, a possibility grid organizes all joint combinations along intersecting coordinate axes. For a card-drawing scenario with cards numbered $1, 2, 3$ drawn in two consecutive trials with replacement:

  • Grid Construction: Rows represent Trial 1 outcomes $\{1, 2, 3\}$ and columns represent Trial 2 outcomes $\{1, 2, 3\}$.
  • Total Possible Outcomes: $3 \times 3 = 9$ total sample combinations.
  • Sample Space Mapping: For tracking the product of two numbers drawn: $$\begin{array}{c|ccc} \times & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ \hline 1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 3 & 3 & 6 & 9 \end{array}$$
  • Favorable Outcomes Extraction: Even products: $\{2, 2, 4, 6, 6\}$ giving 5 favorable paths.
  • Probability Calculation: $P(\text{Even Product}) = \frac{\text{Favorable Outcomes}}{\text{Total Outcomes}} = \frac{5}{9}$

How do we construct probability tree diagrams? For sequential trials extending to three or more stages, a hierarchical tree diagram maps every branching possibility over time:

  • Branch Architecture: Each flip splits the path into two choices: Heads ($H$) or Tails ($T$), each with probability $0.5$.
  • Total Sample Space: Three flips yield $2^3 = 8$ terminal paths: $\{HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT\}$.
  • Single Branch Probability: For three consecutive Tails ($TTT$): $P(TTT) = \frac{1}{8}$
  • Sample Space Size Formula: For $n$ independent trials with $k$ outcomes each, total outcomes $= k^n$.

What are independent events? Two events $A$ and $B$ are statistically independent if the outcome of the first event has no impact on the probability of the second event occurring.

  • Key Property: $P(A|B) = P(A)$ and $P(B|A) = P(B)$
  • Examples of Independent Events:
    • Flipping a coin and rolling a die
    • Drawing a card with replacement
    • Two separate medical tests

What is the Product Rule for independent events? The joint probability of both independent events happening together is the product of their individual probabilities:

  • Formula: $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$
  • Medical Diagnostics Example: If probability of positive on Test A is $0.9$ and on Test B is $0.7$, the probability of positive on both: $$P(\text{Positive on Both}) = 0.9 \times 0.7 = 0.63$$
  • Industrial Quality Control Example: If product passes Check A with probability $0.9$ and Check B with probability $0.8$: $$P(\text{Pass Both}) = 0.9 \times 0.8 = 0.72$$
  • Coin Toss Example: Probability of Heads on three consecutive flips: $$P(HHH) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{8}$$

What are the key probability formulas?

  • Classical Probability: $P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}$
  • Product Rule (Independent Events): $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$
  • Complement Rule: $P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$
  • Addition Rule (Mutually Exclusive): $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$

How do we calculate probability from tree diagrams?

  • Step 1: Identify the path representing the desired outcome.
  • Step 2: Multiply probabilities along each branch of the path.
  • Step 3: If multiple paths lead to the same outcome, add their probabilities.
  • Example: Probability of exactly 2 Heads in 3 coin flips = paths HHT, HTH, THH: $$P(\text{exactly 2H}) = \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{3}{8}$$

What are real-world applications of compound probability?

  • Medical Screening: Calculating the probability of positive results on multiple diagnostic tests.
  • Quality Control: Determining the likelihood of a product passing multiple inspection stages.
  • Insurance: Assessing risk probability for multiple independent events.
  • Weather Forecasting: Calculating probability of rain on consecutive days.

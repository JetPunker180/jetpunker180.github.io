What defines basic statistics? Basic statistics involves collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data to draw meaningful conclusions. Exercise 12.3 introduces measures of statistical dispersion including range, variance, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation - essential tools for understanding data spread and variability in FBISE 2026 board examination.

What are measures of dispersion? Measures of dispersion quantify the extent to which numerical data points scatter or deviate from their central average value. While measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) identify the middle of a dataset, they fail to reveal how spread out the individual points are. The three fundamental absolute metrics are:

  • Range ($R$): The simplest absolute measure, calculated as the direct difference between the maximum and minimum observed values: $$R = \text{Maximum} - \text{Minimum}$$
  • Variance ($S^2$ or $\sigma^2$): The arithmetic mean of the squared deviations taken from the mean. Squaring ensures that positive and negative deviations do not cancel each other out.
  • Standard Deviation ($S$ or $\sigma$): The positive square root of the variance, which brings the spread metric back into the original linear units of the collected data.

How do we calculate measures of dispersion for grouped data? Processing structured interval distributions requires converting ranges into discrete midpoints ($x$) weighted by their respective row frequencies ($f$):

  • Step 1: Find Class Midpoints: For each class interval, calculate the midpoint: $$x = \frac{\text{Lower Limit} + \text{Upper Limit}}{2}$$
  • Step 2: Calculate Weighted Arithmetic Mean ($\bar{x}$ or $\mu$): Multiply each midpoint by its frequency, sum them up, and divide by the total number of items ($N = \sum f$): $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$$
  • Step 3: Calculate Grouped Variance ($S^2$): Using either the mean deviation method or the direct computation formula: $$S^2 = \frac{\sum f(x-\bar{x})^2}{\sum f} = \frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \left(\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\right)^2$$
  • Step 4: Calculate Standard Deviation ($S$): Take the positive square root of the variance: $$S = \sqrt{\frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \left(\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\right)^2}$$

What is the Coefficient of Variation ($CV$)? Absolute standard deviations cannot be directly compared if the datasets use different units or vary widely in scale. The Coefficient of Variation ($CV$) normalizes the standard deviation against the mean, expressing the result as a unitless percentage:

  • Formula: $CV = \left(\frac{S}{\bar{x}}\right) \times 100\%$
  • Interpretation: A higher $CV$ percentage reveals greater relative volatility and lower consistency within that specific sample group.
  • Application: Comparing variability across different measurements (e.g., blood pressure vs. energy consumption).

What are the key formulas for grouped data dispersion?

  • Range: $R = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}$
  • Variance (Mean Deviation Method): $S^2 = \frac{\sum f(x-\bar{x})^2}{\sum f}$
  • Variance (Direct Computation Method): $S^2 = \frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \left(\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\right)^2$
  • Standard Deviation: $S = \sqrt{S^2} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \left(\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\right)^2}$
  • Coefficient of Variation: $CV = \left(\frac{S}{\bar{x}}\right) \times 100\%$

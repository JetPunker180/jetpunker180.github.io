What defines basic statistics? Basic statistics involves collecting, organizing, analyzing, and visualizing data to draw meaningful conclusions. Exercise 12.2 expands on data analysis by introducing Box and Whisker plots (Boxplots), five-number summaries, correlation analysis between variables, and percentile group interpretations - essential tools for FBISE 2026 board examination success.

What is the five-number summary and boxplot architecture? A Box and Whisker plot visually represents a dataset using five key milestones:

  • Minimum Value: The smallest observation in the dataset.
  • First Quartile ($Q_1$): The 25th percentile, marking the lower boundary of the central box.
  • Median ($Q_2$): The 50th percentile, represented by a vertical line inside the box.
  • Third Quartile ($Q_3$): The 75th percentile, marking the upper boundary of the central box.
  • Maximum Value: The largest observation in the dataset.

How do we construct a box and whisker plot? Step-by-step construction process:

  • Step 1: Arrange data in ascending order.
  • Step 2: Find the median ($Q_2$) - the middle value.
  • Step 3: Find $Q_1$ - the median of the lower half of data.
  • Step 4: Find $Q_3$ - the median of the upper half of data.
  • Step 5: Identify the minimum and maximum values.
  • Step 6: Draw a number line scaled to cover the data range.
  • Step 7: Draw the box from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$ with a vertical line at the median.
  • Step 8: Extend whiskers from the box to the minimum and maximum values.

What does the central box represent? The central box drawn from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$ represents the Interquartile Range ($\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$), capturing the middle 50% of the data:

  • Box Width: The horizontal length of the box equals the IQR.
  • Median Line: The vertical line inside the box shows the central tendency.
  • Box Density: A shorter box indicates less variability in the middle 50% of data.

What do the whiskers represent? The whiskers extend from the edges of the box to the minimum and maximum values:

  • Lower Whisker: Extends from $Q_1$ to the minimum value.
  • Upper Whisker: Extends from $Q_3$ to the maximum value.
  • Whisker Length: Shows the spread of the remaining 50% of data (lower 25% and upper 25%).

How do we calculate interquartile range (IQR) across varying scales? The IQR measures statistical dispersion by focusing on the middle 50% of data:

  • Formula: $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$
  • Absolute Value Importance: IQR must always be positive as it measures absolute spatial spread.
  • Example - Car Data:
    • Car Mass: $Q_1 = 1200\text{ kg}$, $Q_3 = 1700\text{ kg}$
    • $\text{IQR}_{\text{Mass}} = 1700 - 1200 = 500\text{ kg}$
  • Example - Fuel Efficiency:
    • Fuel Efficiency: $Q_1 = 20\text{ km/l}$, $Q_3 = 12\text{ km/l}$
    • $\text{IQR}_{\text{Efficiency}} = |12 - 20| = 8\text{ km/l}$ (absolute value)

What is correlation and how do we identify trends? Correlation measures the relationship between two variables:

  • Positive Correlation: As one variable increases, the other increases (e.g., height and weight).
  • Negative Correlation: As one variable increases, the other decreases (e.g., car mass and fuel efficiency).
  • Zero/Neutral Correlation: No relationship exists between the variables.
  • Engineering Application: In vehicle testing, as car mass increases, fuel efficiency consistently drops, creating a negative correlation. This leads to the conclusion that lighter vehicles are more fuel-efficient.

How do we interpret percentile groups using quartile boundaries? Quartiles divide data into four equal parts, each containing exactly 25% of observations:

  • Below $Q_1$: Lowest 25% of data.
  • Between $Q_1$ and $Q_2$: Next 25% of data (25th-50th percentile).
  • Between $Q_2$ and $Q_3$: Next 25% of data (50th-75th percentile).
  • Above $Q_3$: Top 25% of data (75th-100th percentile).
  • Example - Test Scores: If $Q_3 = 85$, then 75% of students scored at or below 85, and 25% scored above 85: $\text{Percentage above } Q_3 = 100\% - 75\% = 25\%$

What are the advantages of box and whisker plots?

  • Visual Summary: Provides a quick visual summary of data distribution.
  • Outlier Detection: Points beyond whiskers are easily identified as outliers.
  • Comparison: Multiple boxplots can be drawn side-by-side for comparison.
  • Symmetry Assessment: Box symmetry indicates data symmetry; skewed boxes indicate skewness.

How is the numerical discriminant extracted from a quadratic equation? For a standard quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, the discriminant is $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$.

Examples:

  • $x^2 + 6x - 27 = 0$: $a=1$, $b=6$, $c=-27$ → $\Delta = 36 - 4(1)(-27) = 36 + 108 = 144$ (positive, two distinct real roots)
  • $8x^2 + 2x + 1 = 0$: $a=8$, $b=2$, $c=1$ → $\Delta = 4 - 4(8)(1) = 4 - 32 = -28$ (negative, two complex conjugate roots)

What is the equal roots condition? For equal roots, $\Delta = 0$. This is used to solve for unknown parameters.

Example - Proving structural constraints: For $(ax+c)^2 = 4bx$, expand to $a^2x^2 + (2ac-4b)x + c^2 = 0$. Setting discriminant to zero: $(2ac-4b)^2 - 4(a^2)(c^2) = 0$. Expand $(2ac-4b)^2 = 4a^2c^2 - 16abc + 16b^2$. Then: $4a^2c^2 - 16abc + 16b^2 - 4a^2c^2 = 0$ → $-16abc + 16b^2 = 0$ → $16b(b - ac) = 0$ → $b = ac$ (for $b \neq 0$).

What is the real roots condition? For real roots, $\Delta \ge 0$.

Example - Parameterized equation: $mx^2 - 2mx + m - 1 = 0$: $\Delta = (-2m)^2 - 4(m)(m-1) = 4m^2 - 4m^2 + 4m = 4m$. For real roots, $4m \ge 0$ → $m \ge 0$.

Geometric interpretation of discriminant:

  • $\Delta > 0$: Two distinct x-axis intersections
  • $\Delta = 0$: One tangent intersection (vertex on x-axis)
  • $\Delta < 0$: No real x-axis intersections (parabola above or below axis)

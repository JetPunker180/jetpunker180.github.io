How do we reduce non-linear polynomial expressions to standard quadratic form? A quadratic equation is a second-degree polynomial equation in a single variable. The standard format is $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are real constants, and $a \neq 0$.

Example: $(x-5)^2 - (2x+4)^2 = 7$ expands and simplifies to $3x^2 + 26x - 2 = 0$.

What is the discriminant and how does it predict the nature of roots? The discriminant is $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$. It determines the nature of roots without solving the equation:

  • $\Delta > 0$: Two distinct real roots
  • $\Delta = 0$: One repeated real root (double root)
  • $\Delta < 0$: Two complex conjugate roots

What is the quadratic formula? $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

What is the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra? A polynomial of degree $n$ has exactly $n$ complex roots (counting multiplicity). Therefore, a quadratic equation ($n=2$) can never have more than two solutions.

How are quadratic equations applied to geometric optimization? Example: An ice skating rink measuring 30 m × 60 m (area 1800 m²) needs to double its area to 3600 m² by adding strips of uniform width $x$ to one side and one end. New dimensions: $(30+x)$ and $(60+x)$. Equation: $(30+x)(60+x) = 3600$ → $x^2 + 90x - 1800 = 0$. Solving gives $x \approx 16.85$ m (discard negative root).

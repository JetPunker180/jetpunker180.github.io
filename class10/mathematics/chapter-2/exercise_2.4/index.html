How do we determine the sum and product of roots directly from a quadratic equation? For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$, Vieta's formulas give:

  • Sum of roots: $S = \alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$
  • Product of roots: $P = \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$

Examples:

  • $x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$: $a=1$, $b=-5$, $c=2$ → $S = -(-5)/1 = 5$, $P = 2/1 = 2$
  • $-4x^2 - 6x - 2 = 0$: $a=-4$, $b=-6$, $c=-2$ → $S = -(-6)/(-4) = 6/(-4) = -3/2$, $P = (-2)/(-4) = 1/2$

How do we construct a new quadratic equation from modified roots? Using the formula $x^2 - Sx + P = 0$, where $S$ is the new sum and $P$ is the new product.

Clearing fractional denominators: For $S = -19$, $P = -\frac{7}{2}$: $x^2 - (-19)x + (-\frac{7}{2}) = x^2 + 19x - \frac{7}{2} = 0$. Multiply by 2: $2x^2 + 38x - 7 = 0$.

Advanced parameter problems: For equation $x^2 - 3kx + 5 = 0$ with roots $S_1$ and $S_1 - S$ (where $S$ is some expression). Set sum of these roots equal to $-b/a$: $(S_1) + (S_1 - S) = 3k$, which simplifies to $2S_1 - S = 3k$. This allows systematic isolation of variable parameters through simultaneous substitution.

How do we classify matrices as singular or non-singular? The determinant of a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ is calculated as $|A| = ad - bc$.

  • If $|A| = 0$, the matrix is singular (no inverse exists).
  • If $|A| \neq 0$, the matrix is non-singular (inverse exists).

What is the adjoint of a $2 \times 2$ matrix? For $A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$, the adjoint is $\text{adj}(A) = \begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix}$ (swap diagonal elements $a$ and $d$, change signs of off-diagonal $b$ and $c$).

How do we find the multiplicative inverse of a matrix? $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A)$. This requires $|A| \neq 0$.

What is Cramer's Rule for solving linear systems? For the system $a_1x + b_1y = c_1$ and $a_2x + b_2y = c_2$:

  • $D = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & b_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 \end{vmatrix} = a_1b_2 - a_2b_1$
  • $D_x = \begin{vmatrix} c_1 & b_1 \\ c_2 & b_2 \end{vmatrix} = c_1b_2 - c_2b_1$
  • $D_y = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & c_2 \end{vmatrix} = a_1c_2 - a_2c_1$
  • $x = \frac{D_x}{D}$, $y = \frac{D_y}{D}$ (requires $D \neq 0$)

System consistency: If $D \neq 0$, the system has a unique solution. If $D = 0$, the system either has no solution (inconsistent) or infinitely many solutions (dependent).

How do we determine the order of a matrix and understand its structural components? A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers arranged in rows and columns. The order of a matrix is denoted as $m \times n$, where $m$ represents the number of rows and $n$ represents the number of columns. Count the horizontal rows and vertical columns systematically.

Example: The matrix $[1+2 \quad 4+5]$ simplifies to $[3 \quad 9]$, having one row and two columns, resulting in an order of $1 \times 2$.

What defines the transpose of a matrix? The transpose of a matrix, denoted $A^T$ or $A^t$, is formed by interchanging the rows and columns of the original matrix $A$. If $A$ is $m \times n$, then $A^T$ is $n \times m$.

Symmetric Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is symmetric if $A^T = A$. This implies $a_{ij} = a_{ji}$ for all $i,j$ (elements reflect across the main diagonal).

Skew-Symmetric Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is skew-symmetric if $A^T = -A$. This implies:

  • Diagonal elements are zero ($a_{ii} = 0$)
  • Off-diagonal elements satisfy $a_{ij} = -a_{ji}$

Example - Symmetric matrix of order $3 \times 3$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 5 \\ 4 & 2 & 6 \\ 5 & 6 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Example - Skew-symmetric matrix of order $3 \times 3$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -2 & 0 & 4 \\ 3 & -4 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

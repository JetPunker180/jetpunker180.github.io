How are complex word problems modeled into systems of linear equations for matrix solutions? Many physical and logical scenarios can be translated into systems of equations with two variables. The core challenge is defining these variables and extracting linear relationships into the standard form $Ax + By = C$. Once in matrix form $AX = B$, Cramer's Rule provides a robust computational path: $x = D_x/D$ and $y = D_y/D$.

Geometry Problems - Triangle Angles: In a right triangle where one acute angle is $10^\circ$ less than the other:

  • Sum of acute angles in a right triangle: $x + y = 90$
  • Difference condition: $x - y = 10$ (or $y = x - 10$)
  • Solve using Cramer's Rule to find the two acute angles.

Perimeter and Dimension Problems: When width is a fractional ratio of length ($W = \frac{1}{4}L$) combined with perimeter formula ($2L + 2W = P$), the system becomes:

  • $W = \frac{1}{4}L$ → $4W = L$ → $L - 4W = 0$
  • $2L + 2W = P$

Mixture Optimization Problems: Combining resources (like soybean meal and corn meal) to meet specific percentage requirements (e.g., 12% protein content):

  • Total weight equation: $x + y = T$
  • Protein concentration equation: $p_1 x + p_2 y = p_T T$

Cramer's Rule for $2 \times 2$ system $a_1x + b_1y = c_1$, $a_2x + b_2y = c_2$:

  • $D = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & b_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 \end{vmatrix} = a_1b_2 - a_2b_1$
  • $D_x = \begin{vmatrix} c_1 & b_1 \\ c_2 & b_2 \end{vmatrix} = c_1b_2 - c_2b_1$
  • $D_y = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & c_1 \\ a_2 & c_2 \end{vmatrix} = a_1c_2 - a_2c_1$
  • $x = D_x/D$, $y = D_y/D$ (requires $D \neq 0$)

Validation: Always check that the computed values satisfy the original physical conditions (e.g., angles must be positive, mixture weights must be non-negative).

What is the Kinetic Particle Theory? The Kinetic Particle Theory explains the behavior of matter by stating that all particles (atoms, molecules, ions) are in constant random motion. The amount of kinetic energy determines whether a substance exists as a solid, liquid, or gas.

Why does temperature remain constant during phase changes? During phase transitions like melting, boiling, or freezing, the heat energy supplied is used as latent heat to break or form intermolecular bonds rather than increasing the kinetic energy of the particles. This is why temperature remains constant during phase changes.

What is sublimation? Sublimation is the direct transition of a solid into a gas without passing through the liquid state. Examples: dry ice (solid CO₂), iodine, camphor, naphthalene.

What is boiling point? The boiling point is the temperature at which a liquid's vapor pressure equals the external (atmospheric) pressure. At this point, bubbles of vapor form throughout the liquid.

What is Charles's Law? Charles's Law states that at constant pressure, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature: $V \propto T$ or $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$.

What is Avogadro's Law? Avogadro's Law states that at constant temperature and pressure, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to the number of moles (or number of particles): $V \propto n$.

How does molecular mass affect diffusion? According to Graham's Law of Diffusion, the rate of diffusion is inversely proportional to the square root of the molecular mass: $r \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$. Lighter gases diffuse faster than heavier gases.

How does temperature affect diffusion? As temperature increases, particles gain more kinetic energy and move faster, so the rate of diffusion increases with temperature.

How does altitude affect the boiling point of water? At higher altitudes, atmospheric pressure is lower, so water reaches its boiling point at a lower temperature (e.g., 93°C at 2000 meters). This significantly prolongs cooking times because food cooks at a lower temperature.

What are heating and cooling curves? Heating and cooling curves show temperature changes as heat is added or removed. The flat (horizontal) regions on these curves represent phase changes where kinetic energy (KE) remains constant but potential energy (PE) changes as intermolecular bonds are broken or formed.

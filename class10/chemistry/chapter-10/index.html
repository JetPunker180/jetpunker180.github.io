What are hydroxy compounds? Hydroxy compounds are organic compounds containing one or more hydroxyl (-OH) groups attached to a carbon atom. Alcohols are the most common hydroxy compounds, with ethanol being the most important example.

What organism and conditions are required for ethanol fermentation? Ethanol is produced using yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) to convert sugars into alcohol and carbon dioxide at an optimal temperature of 25-35°C under anaerobic (oxygen-free) conditions. This is a slow biological process using renewable biomass.

What is the chemical equation for fermentation? C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) → 2C₂H₅OH (ethanol) + 2CO₂ (carbon dioxide)

How does catalytic hydration differ from fermentation? While fermentation uses renewable biomass, catalytic hydration produces ethanol by reacting ethene (derived from petroleum) with steam using a phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) catalyst at high temperature (300°C) and pressure (60-70 atm). This method is fast but energy-intensive and petroleum-dependent.

Why is ethanol preferred as a ''cleaner'' alternative to fossil fuels? Ethanol is carbon-neutral because the CO₂ released during its combustion is offset by the CO₂ absorbed by plants during their growth, resulting in a lower net contribution to global warming. It is a renewable fuel produced from crops that can be replanted and grown cyclically.

What are the industrial applications of ethanol?

| Application | Role | |-------------|------| | Fuel | Renewable, carbon-neutral alternative to gasoline | | Solvent | Dissolves many organic compounds | | Antiseptic | Kills bacteria on skin (hand sanitizers, wipes) | | Preservative | Preserves biological specimens and some medications | | Beverage | Alcoholic drinks |

What roles do alcohols play in the cosmetic and household sectors? They act as astringents in skin care (tightening pores), help dissolve and mix ingredients to prevent separation in perfumes and lotions (emulsifiers), and serve as effective disinfectants in household cleaners.

Comparison: Fermentation vs Catalytic Hydration

| Feature | Fermentation | Catalytic Hydration | |---------|--------------|---------------------| | Raw material | Renewable biomass (sugars, crops) | Non-renewable petroleum (ethene) | | Catalyst | Enzymes (yeast) | Phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) | | Temperature | 25-35°C | 300°C | | Pressure | Atmospheric | 60-70 atm | | Speed | Slow (days) | Fast (continuous) | | Byproduct | CO₂ | None | | Carbon footprint | Lower (carbon-neutral) | Higher |

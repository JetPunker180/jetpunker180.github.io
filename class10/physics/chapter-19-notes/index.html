How can we detect and define the magnetic effects of an electric current? The presence of a magnetic field is detected using a magnetic compass. A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field that is always perpendicular to the direction of current flow. The strength of the magnetic field around a wire is directly proportional to the magnitude of the current passing through it.

What are the rules for determining direction?

  • Right-Hand Grip Rule: Thumb points in the direction of current, curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field lines around the conductor.
  • Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Thumb = Force/Motion, Index finger = Magnetic Field (North to South), Middle finger = Current (positive to negative). Used to find direction of force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

What is electromagnetic induction? Electromagnetic induction is the process of generating an electromotive force (e.m.f.) across a conductor when it is exposed to a changing magnetic field. The induced e.m.f. is given by ε = 2NvBL sinθ, where N is number of turns, v is speed, B is magnetic field strength, L is length of conductor, θ is angle.

What factors influence the strength of an induced e.m.f.? The induced e.m.f. is directly proportional to:

  • The rate of change of magnetic flux
  • The number of turns (N) in the coil
  • The magnetic field strength (B)
  • The speed of relative motion (v)

What is Lenz's Law? Lenz's Law states that the direction of an induced current always opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. This is a consequence of the conservation of energy.

What are the primary energy transformations in electromagnetic devices?

| Device | Energy Conversion | Direction | |--------|------------------|-----------| | Electric motor | Electrical → Mechanical | Rotational motion | | Electric generator | Mechanical → Electrical | Electromagnetic induction |

What is the difference between AC and DC generators?

| Feature | AC Generator | DC Generator | |---------|-------------|--------------| | Output | Alternating current | Direct current | | Ring type | Slip rings (two continuous rings) | Split rings (commutator) | | Current direction | Reverses every half cycle | Remains constant |

How do transformers work? Transformers are static devices that transfer energy between circuits through mutual inductance. They change voltage levels while keeping power and frequency constant in an ideal transformer.

Why is high voltage essential for long-distance power transmission? Power loss in transmission lines is P_loss = I²R. By transmitting power at high voltage, the current is reduced. Since power loss is proportional to the square of the current, lower current significantly minimizes energy wasted as heat in overhead cables.

Why are transformers ineffective with DC voltage? Transformers require a constantly changing magnetic field to induce voltage in the secondary coil. AC naturally provides this variation, while DC produces a constant, unchanging magnetic field that cannot facilitate mutual induction.

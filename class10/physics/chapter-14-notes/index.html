What are the Laws of Reflection? The Laws of Reflection state that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray, and normal all lie in the same plane.

What are the characteristics of an image formed by a plane mirror? The image is virtual, upright, the same size as the object, laterally inverted (left and right sides reversed), and appears at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.

What is refraction? Refraction is the bending of light rays that occurs when light changes speed and direction as it moves from one medium to another.

Why is the index of refraction always greater than or equal to unity? The refractive index n = c/v, where c is the speed of light in vacuum (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s) and v is the speed in the medium. Since light travels fastest in a vacuum, c/v is always ≥ 1.

What are the conditions for Total Internal Reflection (TIR)? TIR occurs when: (1) light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium, and (2) the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle (θc).

What is dispersion? Dispersion occurs when white light passes through a prism and separates into its seven constituent colors (ROYGBIV: Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet) because different wavelengths bend at different angles.

How are common vision defects corrected using lenses?

| Defect | Condition | Corrected with | |--------|-----------|----------------| | Myopia (Short-sightedness) | Can see near objects clearly, distant objects blurry | Diverging (concave) lenses | | Hyperopia (Long-sightedness) | Can see distant objects clearly, near objects blurry | Converging (convex) lenses |

What is the difference between real and virtual images?

| Feature | Real Image | Virtual Image | |---------|------------|---------------| | Can be projected on screen | Yes | No | | Light rays | Actually pass through the image | Appear to diverge from the image | | Example | Image from convex lens | Image from plane mirror |

What are gravitational and acoustic lensing?

  • Gravitational lensing: Uses the curvature of space-time from massive objects (like galaxies) to bend light from distant sources.
  • Acoustic lensing: Redirects sound waves by creating variations in acoustic impedance (similar to how optical lenses bend light).

What is thermal expansion? Thermal expansion is the tendency of matter to change its volume in response to a change in temperature. When a substance is heated, its particles move more vigorously, increasing the average distance between them.

What is linear expansion? Linear expansion refers to the increase in length of a solid when heated. The change in length ΔL = α L₀ ΔT, where α is the coefficient of linear expansion, L₀ is original length, and ΔT is temperature change.

What is the relationship between linear and volume expansion? The coefficient of volume expansion (β) is three times the coefficient of linear expansion (α): β = 3α or α = β/3.

Why does temperature remain constant during melting or boiling? During phase transitions (melting, boiling), the energy supplied is used as latent heat to break or form intermolecular bonds rather than increasing the kinetic energy of the molecules. This is why temperature remains constant.

What is latent heat? Latent heat refers to ''hidden'' energy absorbed or released during a change of state with no change in temperature. Latent heat of fusion is for solid↔liquid, latent heat of vaporization is for liquid↔gas.

What is the difference between evaporation and boiling?

| Feature | Evaporation | Boiling | |---------|-------------|---------| | Location | Only at surface | Throughout liquid | | Temperature | Any temperature | Fixed boiling point | | Speed | Slow | Rapid | | Heat source | Absorbs heat from surroundings | Requires continuous external heat |

What factors affect the rate of evaporation? The rate of evaporation is directly proportional to temperature, surface area, and wind speed, and inversely proportional to humidity. For example, dry air allows more water vapor to be absorbed than humid air (cooling effect).

What is the difference between real and apparent expansion of liquids? Real expansion considers the actual increase in liquid volume including the expansion of the container, while apparent expansion is the observed increase without accounting for the container expansion.

How do bimetallic strips function in temperature control? They consist of two bonded metals with different expansion rates. When temperature changes, the strip bends due to unequal expansion, which can be used to make or break electrical connections in thermostats, thermometers, and safety switches.

What is superconductivity? Superconductivity is a phenomenon where certain materials exhibit exactly zero electrical resistance and the expulsion of magnetic fields (Meissner effect) at very low temperatures (below critical temperature).

What is the Joule-Thomson effect? The Joule-Thomson effect is the change in temperature of a gas when it expands rapidly through a valve or porous plug. This rapid expansion causes cooling, as seen in fire extinguishers and refrigeration systems.

What are heating curves? Heating curves show temperature changes as heat is added. The horizontal plateau stages indicate phase changes (melting, boiling) where temperature remains constant.

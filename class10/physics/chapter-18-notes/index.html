What is the difference between N-type and P-type semiconductors?

  • N-type materials: Created by adding pentavalent impurities (donors) such as phosphorus or arsenic. These provide extra free electrons as majority charge carriers.
  • P-type materials: Created by adding trivalent impurities (acceptors) such as boron or aluminum. These create "holes" as positive charge carriers.

How does biasing affect the depletion region of a diode?

  • Forward biasing: P-side connected to positive, N-side to negative. This decreases the width of the depletion region, allowing current to flow.
  • Reverse biasing: P-side connected to negative, N-side to positive. This increases the width of the depletion region, effectively blocking current flow.

What is the potential barrier for silicon and germanium diodes? The potential barrier is the voltage required to overcome the depletion region. Silicon: approximately 0.7 V, Germanium: approximately 0.3 V.

What are the characteristics of a transistor? A transistor has three terminals: emitter (E), base (B), and collector (C).

  • Emitter: Heavily doped to provide charge carriers.
  • Base: Thinnest and lightly doped to minimize recombination.
  • Collector: Largest region to collect carriers.

How does a transistor function as a switch? A transistor acts as a switch by using a small voltage or current at the base terminal to control a much larger flow of current between the emitter and collector. When the base voltage is high, it is "ON" (closed switch), and when low, it is "OFF" (open switch).

What is the fundamental current relationship in a transistor? I_E = I_B + I_C (emitter current = base current + collector current). The current gain (β) is the ratio of collector current to base current: β = I_C/I_B.

What are logic gates? Logic gates are electronic circuits that perform logical operations on one or more binary inputs to produce a single binary output.

| Gate | Symbol | Output | |------|--------|--------| | AND | High only when ALL inputs are high | A · B | | OR | High when ANY input is high | A + B | | NOT | Inverts the input | Ā | | NAND | NOT AND (universal gate) | (A·B)' | | NOR | NOT OR (universal gate) | (A+B)' | | XOR | High when inputs are different | A ⊕ B |

What are universal gates? NAND and NOR gates are called universal gates because they can be combined to implement any other logic function (AND, OR, NOT). This makes them essential in digital circuit design.

Why are digital systems generally preferred over analog systems? Digital systems represent information in discrete values (0s and 1s), providing higher accuracy, better noise immunity, and easier data storage compared to the continuous signals of analog systems.

How do solid-state relays (SSR) improve upon mechanical relays? SSRs use semiconductors and optocouplers for switching instead of moving parts, resulting in faster speeds, silent operation, and a longer lifespan due to the absence of mechanical wear.

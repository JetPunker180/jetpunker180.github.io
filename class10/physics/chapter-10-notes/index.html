What is specific heat capacity? Specific heat capacity is the heat energy required to raise the temperature of one unit mass of a substance by one degree Celsius. Formula: Q = mcΔT, where Q is heat energy, m is mass, c is specific heat capacity, and ΔT is temperature change.

Why is water an ideal coolant for automobile radiators? Water has a large specific heat capacity (4184 J/kg°C), meaning it can absorb a considerable amount of heat from the engine without a drastic rise in its own temperature.

What is conduction? Conduction is the transfer of heat through direct contact where energy is passed via vibrations and collisions of particles. Metals are superior conductors because they possess a ''sea'' of free electrons that move rapidly to enhance energy transfer.

What is convection? Convection is the transfer of heat through the movement of fluids (liquids and gases) driven by density differences. Warm fluids rise (less dense) and cool fluids sink (more dense), creating convection currents. This is the primary driving force behind mantle convection which moves tectonic plates.

Which mode of heat transfer is the fastest? Radiation is the fastest method because it involves electromagnetic waves that travel at the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s) and does not require a physical medium to transfer energy.

What is the greenhouse effect? Greenhouse gases like CO₂ and methane trap infrared radiation in the atmosphere, leading to global warming. This provides more energy for intense hurricanes and more frequent heat waves.

Comparison of heat transfer modes:

| Mode | Medium Required | Speed | Mechanism | Example | |------|----------------|-------|-----------|---------| | Conduction | Solid/Liquid | Slow | Particle vibrations, free electrons | Metal rod heating | | Convection | Fluids (liquids/gases) | Moderate | Density-driven currents | Boiling water, AC | | Radiation | No medium | Fastest (speed of light) | Electromagnetic waves | Sun heating Earth |

What is latent heat of vaporization? The latent heat of vaporization of water is 2260 kJ/kg. This explains why 1 kg of steam releases significantly more energy than 1 kg of hot water when cooling from 100°C to 20°C.

Effective Time Management Techniques in NET - Engineering

Published on April 11, 2026 • By NotesPrep Team
Preparing for the NET is not just about academic knowledge—it is about execution under strict time constraints. With 200 questions to be solved in 180 minutes, your approach must be deliberate, structured, and efficient. Every minute carries weight, and how you allocate your time across sections can significantly influence your final score.

Understand the Time Pressure

The exam effectively gives you less than a minute per question. This makes it essential to avoid treating the NET like a traditional board exam. Instead of aiming for perfect solutions, the focus should be on maximizing correct attempts within limited time.

Section-Wise Strategy

Each Section in NUST Entry Test require a different approach. If you waste majority of your time on English, you would surely struggle calculating complex Mathematical questions. We are going to share with you a tested and really helpful time management strategy at NUST, that will for sure leads you to success. A disciplined sequence can improve both speed and accuracy and promise you a score above 145.

Start with English (First Priority)

English is the most time-efficient section. It requires no calculations and relies largely on instinct and familiarity with language.
  • Aim to solve 2-3 questions per minute.
  • If you know the answer, select it immediately.
  • If unsure, choose the closest or most natural option.
  • Avoid overthinking; hesitation here wastes valuable time.

This section should be completed quickly to build momentum and secure easy marks.

Move to Physics (Second Priority)

Physics involves some calculation, but it is generally less intensive than Math.

  • Spend no more than 40–50 seconds per question.
  • Focus on conceptual questions first.
  • If a question looks lengthy or calculation-heavy, mark it for review and skip.
  • Return to difficult questions only after completing the rest.

This selective approach ensures that straightforward marks are not sacrificed for time-consuming problems.

Leave Mathematics for Last (Core Phase)

Mathematics is the most demanding section and should be attempted when your earlier sections are complete.

  • Allocate around 100–110 minutes.
  • Use a quick decision filter: if you cannot identify a solution path within 10–15 seconds, skip.
  • Avoid spending more than a minute on any single question unless you are confident.
  • Focus on solvable questions first, then revisit flagged ones.

This prevents getting stuck early and losing time across multiple questions.

The Importance of Attempting Every Question.

One of the biggest advantages in the NET is the absence of negative marking.

  • Never leave a question unanswered.
  • If time is running out, make an educated guess.
  • Even random guesses provide a statistical chance of gaining marks.

An unanswered question guarantees zero, while an attempt keeps the possibility open.

The Final 10 Minutes: Review Phase

Reserve the last 10 minutes strictly for review.

  • Ensure every question is attempted
  • Revisit flagged questions quickly
  • Avoid starting entirely new, complex problems

This phase is about maximizing completion, not solving from scratch.

Exam Environment Awareness

The NET is computer-based, and no calculators or electronic devices are allowed inside the hall. However, arrangements are typically available outside for storing personal items. Since calculators are not permitted, questions are designed to be solved through logical shortcuts rather than lengthy calculations. Practicing this mindset beforehand is essential.

Practice with Mock Tests

Since the exam is digital, practicing through mock tests can significantly improve familiarity with the interface, timing, and pressure. Simulating real test conditions helps refine pacing and decision-making. We offer NET Mock Tests for free based on past MCQs. You can find them here.

Target Score and Competitiveness

For most competitive programs, especially in fields like engineering and computing, a score in the range of 145–150 is generally considered strong. Reviewing the latest merit lists can give a clearer idea of the required benchmarks for your desired department.

Final Thoughts

The NET is a test of strategy as much as knowledge. Success depends on knowing when to attempt, when to skip, and how to distribute your effort across sections. A structured approach—fast English, controlled Physics, and focused Mathematics—combined with smart guessing and disciplined time management, can significantly improve your outcome.

Approach the exam with a plan, execute it consistently, and let efficiency work in your favor.
